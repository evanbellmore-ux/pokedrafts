# Pokémon damage calculator

The signed-in `/calculator` route compares two builds under one selected game's rules, with attacks in either direction. Each Pokémon has four prepared quick moves, while **Moves** ranks the current attacking Pokémon's full resolved learnset for that game. The left and right Pokémon are positions, not a permanent attacking/defending role or team ownership. It is a damage calculator, not a battle simulator or a regulation/team-legality validator.

## Game profiles and spread encoding

**Battle game** applies to both Pokémon. The calculator starts in Champions; selecting another game requires confirmation and resets preparation only after its bundled catalog loads successfully.

| Battle game | Engine generation | Pinned Showdown resolution | Training | Available transformation controls |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Pokémon Champions (`champions`) | `0`, Champions dispatch | `champions → base` | Fixed level 50, Stat Points | Source-verified Mega forms, including Champions variants |
| Ultra Sun / Ultra Moon (`ultra_sun_ultra_moon`) | `7` | `gen7 → gen8 → base` | Native level, EVs and IVs | Mega, Primal, Ultra Burst; eligible damaging Z-Moves |
| Sword / Shield (`sword_shield`) | `8` | `gen8 → base` | Native level, EVs and IVs | Dynamax and source-verified Gigantamax |
| Scarlet / Violet (`scarlet_violet`) | `9` | `gen9` aliases `base` | Native level, EVs and IVs | Ordinary Tera; Stellar with explicit first-use context; Ogerpon and Terapagos Tera forms |

Profile capability is not a promise that every interaction is supported. Species, items, moves, abilities, weather and transformation prerequisites are validated against the selected profile; unsupported combinations are explained, not silently calculated as ordinary attacks. The current Champions profile does **not** enable Z-Moves, Dynamax/Gigantamax or Tera.

**Battle game and PokéPaste Spread format are independent.** Battle game selects rules/data for the entire matchup; Spread format says how the source text encodes training. Neither small `EVs:` values, a `[gen7...]`/`[gen9...]` team heading, a Tera field, nor a league's Pool Builder game/regulation changes the selected battle game. Source headings remain informational hints. Native games require original traditional EVs/IVs, never guessed reverse conversions from Champions points.

Fresh manual and species-only league builds use **level 50** in every profile. A native imported set instead preserves an explicit level **1–100**, or uses Showdown's **level 100** default when omitted. Imported defaults are disclosed in the preview; switching games never converts an already-edited build in place.

## Using the calculator

- Open **Calculator** in the app navigation. The **1v1 · 2v2** switch sits under the page heading, with **1v1** pressed on every load; this section describes 1v1, and [2v2](#2v2) describes the other view. **Battle game** sits below the switch and above the **My team · Moves · Opponent** tabs, with **Moves** selected initially. Default matchup: Champions Charizard versus Blastoise at level 50, Doubles, no training investment, full HP. The active Pokémon, types, current/max HP, quick moves and the damage-roll choice, then each Pokémon's **Build settings** and **Field conditions**, remain above the selected pane. Quick slots are populated, but no attack is selected automatically.
- Each summary card has **four quick-move slots** below its **Change Pokémon** and **Edit HP** controls, with that Pokémon's collapsible **Build settings** right below the summary, under its card. Clicking a slot immediately selects its owner as the attacking Pokémon and its move against the other Pokémon, without swapping cards, builds, roster ownership or side conditions. It also opens the existing **Moves** pane in replacement mode for exactly that owner's slot. An empty slot opens the same picker with no active move.
- Replacement candidates come only from that owner's exact resolved learnset in the selected game, excluding all moves already assigned to its four slots, including the slot being edited. **Replace** changes only the targeted slot, selects the new move and updates its damage/HP preview while keeping that slot editable. Focus returns to the updated quick slot. The new move is hidden from candidates because it is now assigned; the old move becomes available again if unassigned. Actual HP and the attacking owner are unchanged. Champions preserves hit counts; native replacements clear that owner's move contexts, including hits, Z activation and Stellar first-use choices. Clicking a row or opening **Details** does not replace anything. **Done** or Escape closes replacement mode while keeping the prepared moves and selected calculation. Outside replacement mode, the normal full-learnset selection controls only preview moves and never rewrite quick slots.
- **Choose a move** using a quick slot or the normal full-learnset selection control, then choose **Low**, **Average** or **High** under **Damage roll**. Average is selected initially. When a safe preview is available, the receiving Pokémon's large HP number and health bar show the HP that chosen roll leaves, whether it is on the left or right, with a lighter band on the bar from the least to the most HP any roll sequence leaves; the HP the move starts from stays visible underneath, or, when the Pokémon ate a berry before the move, that fact ("Sitrus Berry: 91 → 136 HP"). Each card's bar starts from the HP its Pokémon has when the move starts, and the results header names the receiving Pokémon's ("(136 current HP)"); the HP editor keeps the HP as entered. The summary retains the full damage range and the engine's all-roll one-use KO chance, with **Uses to KO** on its own line; the roll choice changes only the HP preview, not either of them. Selecting a move or roll never applies damage to either build.
- **Low** uses minimum damage and leaves the most HP; **High** uses maximum damage and leaves the least. **Average** uses the mean of all 16 damage rolls (including repeated values), rounded once to whole HP. Fixed damage is the same for all choices. The roll choice survives move/build/roster changes and Swap; Reset or reloading returns it to Average.
- Use **Change Pokémon** in the summary to open the chooser directly over your current view, without changing tabs at any screen size. Search **All Pokémon**, or use **Team Pokémon** when the targeted Pokémon's team has usable roster entries. Every typed word must appear in the form's search text, which holds its Showdown name, id and base species, the calculator's catalog aliases (**Mega Charizard X**, **Alolan Raichu**), gender words for -F/-M forms (**Meowstic Female**) and the league roster names that resolve to it (**Paldean Tauros (Blaze Breed)**, **Floette (Eternal Flower)**), so any name the roster lookup accepts is findable (`app/lib/battle/species-search.ts`, checked for every Pool Builder row in all four games by `tests/unit/calculator-species-search.test.ts`). Search results are a filter, not an identity: selecting a result chooses that exact form. Team selections restore that roster entry's session prep; manually selecting a different species resets its build and quick moves. Selecting the current species or active roster entry preserves edits.
- **Mega** buttons beside each summary name change eligible forms in place, without opening Build settings or moving the cards. Eligible variants appear as **Mega**, **Mega X**, **Mega Y** or **Mega Z**; USUM also exposes verified **Primal** and **Ultra Burst** transitions. Clicking a different variant switches directly; clicking the active variant again returns to its entry form. The exact form's ability, required item, types, stats and damage update together. Training, stages, status, actual HP (including unfinished input) and prepared moves stay with that Pokémon. Champions retains hit counts; native form transitions clear its move contexts. Returning to base restores the original ability/activation and held item without undoing other build edits. If the form was selected directly, entry-form defaults are used on the first return. Roster ownership and the active shortcut are preserved, including after refreshing or returning to that roster entry.
- **Edit HP** reveals a compact current-HP input inside that summary card, without opening Build settings. Edits apply immediately to the same build used by Build settings and the calculations. **Done**, Enter or Escape closes the input without undoing or normalizing edits and returns focus to Edit HP. Blank means full HP; nonempty invalid text remains visible and pauses results. The input always edits actual current HP, never the projected HP after damage. The responsive summary (names, HP, mechanic buttons and quick moves) stays sticky only when there is enough vertical space for both it and a usable portion of the page below; otherwise, including when wrapped controls make it too tall, it uses normal scrolling. Build settings and Field conditions sit below it in normal page flow, never inside the pinned box, so opening, closing or changing their controls never pins, unpins or scrolls the page.
- The far-left **My team** and far-right **Opponent** tabs independently choose **League team** or **PokéPaste**. In league mode, My team chooses your team across your leagues, labelled **team name — league name**, and Opponent offers other teams from that selected league. If My team uses a paste, Opponent also exposes a league selector. Changing the league clears its old opponent until the new members load, without replacing an imported team. No opponent or active Pokémon is chosen automatically.
- At desktop widths of 1280px and above, vertical team lists flank the calculator body beneath the full-width heading and tabs: the left Pokémon's team on the left and the right Pokémon's on the right. **Your team** and **Opponent's team** remain separate ownership labels, independent of position or attack direction. Each Pokémon is a full-width shortcut with a sprite and Active indicator. Below 1280px, shortcuts stay inside each Pokémon's **Build settings**. Click a roster Pokémon to make it active. Loading a league or opponent never overwrites the current builds.
- **Build settings** sits right under the summary, below each Pokémon's card and four quick moves: side by side, lined up with the two cards, where the calculator is at least 44rem wide, otherwise one after the other in card order, each header naming its Pokémon. Each starts collapsed and is a disclosure button (Enter/Space, `aria-expanded`/`aria-controls`) whose header shows that Pokémon's issue count (**2 settings to check**) while collapsed. It opens by itself when its Pokémon gains a build issue, and whenever Fix settings, a required-move fix, a roster shortcut moving into it or another shortcut targets it; collapsing it again is left to you. Its open state follows the Pokémon, like its build, through Swap; Reset and game changes start collapsed. Collapsed editors stay mounted, so unfinished numeric text survives. Inside, current HP, nature, available ability/item, status and the applicable level, Dynamax, Gigantamax and Hidden Power fields share one dense grid, followed by the item-timing and ability switches, training and stages, then gender and happiness below the stats. Champions has Stat Points; native games have level, EVs and IVs. The card above it already shows the name, types, Mimicry, Tera type and retained foreign configuration, so the editor does not repeat them, and a Pokémon chosen by hand has no provenance label (a roster Pokémon's card shows **Your team** or **Opponent's team**). Use **Change Pokémon** in the card to search species/forms in a dialog. Selecting a different Pokémon closes the chooser and resets its build and quick moves; selecting the current Pokémon or cancelling preserves its edits. A form with one required item selects and locks it; forms with allowed alternatives must hold one of those exact catalog items.
- **Field conditions** sits directly below both Build settings, across their full width, and collapses the same way: it starts collapsed, its header shows the active-field summary (**Doubles · No weather · No terrain · 1 toggles on**) and its issue count, and it opens by itself when the field gains an issue or when Fix settings targets it. Its open state survives Swap; Reset and game changes start it collapsed. Inside, it exposes Singles/Doubles, weather/terrain, shared effects, each side's screens, Helping Hand, partner abilities, Protect, Tailwind and Charge (each side named after its Pokémon) and critical hits, then the battle states it cannot represent. Weather/terrain are explicit state, not automatically set by entry abilities. Changing Singles/Doubles recalculates damage but never overwrites either Pokémon's prepared moves.
- Moves are sorted by descending minimum damage, then maximum damage, then name; a random hit count compares its expected damage over its counts instead (each count's chance times the sum of its hits' lowest, or highest, rolls: Bullet Seed's 2–5 hits weigh 3.1 hits), not its fewest or most hits. **Uses to KO (fewest first)** orders calculated moves by the guaranteed count instead (ties: the higher chance that one use fewer is enough, then minimum damage, then name), then moves that can knock out without a guarantee (the higher chance first, a one-use move's KO chance against the chance of a KO within the uses counted, then the fewest uses), then those with no KO within the uses counted, those that never KO, deal no damage or are not estimated; uncalculated moves stay last. Search/filter/sort without changing the selected move or its current preview. Move search ignores case, punctuation and spacing and needs every typed word, so `uturn`, `u turn` and `U-turn` all find U-turn and `double edge` finds Double-Edge; it also matches a Z-Move or Max Move name. **Details** holds base power, accuracy, reasons, assumptions, every Uses to KO line with what the count carries into later uses and leaves out, and rolls. All learnset entries remain accounted for, including statuses and unsupported moves.
- A variable-hit move with no count chosen is a calculated row like any other (its random range, below), so selecting it opens nothing. Only a chosen count outside the move's range (Loaded Dice picked up after choosing 2 hits) waits for a count: selecting that move for a normal preview opens its existing hit editor without changing your filters, and its row and the summary offer **Set hits**. Replacing a slot with such a move instead keeps that slot selected/editable and returns focus to the quick slot, without automatically navigating into hit settings. Use the summary's **Set hits**, or close replacement with **Done** before opening its normal **Set hits** control; either path retains the selected move. Hit contexts belong to the attacking owner and move, not a shared move ID: switching attack direction retains each owner's count, even when both Pokémon know the same move. The summary's **Set hits** and **Show move** actions leave replacement mode without clearing the preview, activate **Moves**, then reveal the existing editor, including when already expanded or filtered out. **Show selected move** inside normal move browsing also reveals a filtered-out row; no second hit-count editor is created.
- **Swap** exchanges builds, configured/active transformations, prepared moves, roster shortcuts and side conditions, preserves the active tab, shared field conditions and which settings sections are open (each Build settings follows its Pokémon), and clears the active attack, replacement mode and both owners' move contexts. **Reset** keeps the selected game, returns to **Moves**, restores its initial level-50 matchup/default quick moves and clears session prep, while retaining team-source choices, original imported teams and raw import drafts. The active attack, replacement mode and move contexts also clear on another roster activation, manual species change, account change, and league/opponent/provenance changes; normal build/field edits and unchanged refreshes retain them. Game-switch and account rules are detailed below.

Only one menu pane is visible, but all three remain mounted. Switching tabs preserves unfinished numeric text, Pokémon selection/search, build edits, move query/filter/sort/details, hit counts, field settings, and the selected damage roll. Each chooser starts closed and retains its search and page when reopened. Opening it moves focus into the dialog, and Tab/Shift+Tab wrap within it. Escape/Cancel or clicking its backdrop closes it and returns focus to the summary card's Change Pokémon button that opened it. The summary and Build settings share exact HP text, including unfinished invalid text and leading zeros; closing the inline editor, switching tabs or swapping sides does not discard it. Coverage under Moves starts collapsed. Tab changes do not fetch rosters or activate Pokémon. Desktop shortcuts remain outside the panes; below 1280px they relocate into each Pokémon's Build settings without remounting the editors. If a focused roster shortcut moves there, that Build settings opens before restoring focus. Resizing with focus elsewhere does not change tabs or steal focus.

Use **Left/Right** to move between tabs (wrapping at either end), **Home/End** for the first/last tab, and **Tab** to leave the tab row. Tabs activate on focus and also support native Enter/Space activation; Up/Down retain normal scrolling. Hidden panes are removed from keyboard navigation and accessibility exposure. On narrow screens the tab row and any overflowed stat table scroll inside their own containers; the page keeps normal vertical scrolling. Selecting a tab keeps focus on it, while explicit editor shortcuts move focus into their destination.

Calculator loading/error/invalid-setting feedback stays outside the panes, with **Fix settings** or **Retry calculator**. Each Build settings header and the Field conditions header flag their issue counts even when collapsed. Fix settings opens the affected Pokémon's Build settings (or Field conditions) and focuses an invalid control, including one kept in the card such as its Tera type. Team-loading failures have a separate retry path and do not disable manual calculations.

### Training stats

Champions uses level **50** and **Stat Points**, not conventional EV/IV inputs: 0–32 per stat, at most 66 total. Training stats are:

```text
HP = base + points + 75                 (base-HP-1 exception preserved)
Other = floor((base + points + 20) × nature)
Nature = 1.1, 0.9 or 1
```

In every game's stat table, **Total** (right of **Stage**) is the stat with its stage applied, as pinned Showdown does: floor(stat × (2 + stage) / 2) when raised and floor(stat × 2 / (2 − stage)) when lowered, stages clamped to ±6 (`app/(app)/calculator/stage-stat.ts`); HP has no stage. It updates with the stage and training, and leaves other modifiers (items, abilities, weather, Tailwind) to the calculation.

The adapter maps Champions Stat Points directly into the generation-0 engine parameter named `evs`; it does not convert EVs there. The optional traditional-spread importer converts before creating a Champions build, as described below.

Native builds retain level **1–100**, EVs **0–252 per stat / 510 total**, and effective IVs **0–31**. Fresh manual/league builds use level 50, zero EVs and 31 IVs (in Ultra Sun/Ultra Moon, a form whose quick moves include a typed Hidden Power gets that type's usual IVs instead; see Hidden Power below). Their stats use the selected game's base stats and the conventional level-scaled EV/IV formula, including the base-HP-1 exception. The table displays training stats before stages, abilities, items and Dynamax. Blank, fractional, out-of-range or over-budget training stays invalid instead of being clamped or silently converted.

In USUM, **Specify innate IVs before Hyper Training** exposes separate original IVs for Hidden Power. Enabling it initially copies the displayed IVs; those are editable starting values, not evidence of the original spread. If effective and innate IVs differ, the effective value must be 31 and the build must meet the game's Hyper Training level threshold (100 in generations 7/8, 50 in generation 9). Without separate innate context, the provided IVs are treated as innate. Pasted IVs are never automatically rewritten to make a declared Hidden Power type work.

## 2v2

**2v2** calculates one turn of a Doubles battle: your two Pokémon and the opponent's two each choose a move and, where the move takes one, a target, and every card shows that Pokémon's HP after all four moves, so two attacks into the same slot combine. It is a pressed-button group (`role="group"`, `aria-pressed`), not a tab, since the menus stay the page's only tablist. The view is not persisted: the page opens in 1v1.

| Shared by 1v1 and 2v2 (one state) | 2v2 only |
| --- | --- |
| Battle game; the **My team · Moves · Opponent** menus; team sources (league teams, PokéPaste), roster lists and the Pokémon chooser; session build edits (one roster entry is one prepared set in both views); **Damage roll** | The four Pokémon, their moves and targets, Charge, the 2v2 field, the Moves pane's Pokémon, replacement mode, Intimidate results, notices and which settings sections are open |

- **Slots.** **Your left**, **Your right**, **Opponent's left** and **Opponent's right** are screen positions. From a Pokémon, its targets are **Left foe**, **Right foe**, **Ally** and **Itself**. In pinned Showdown the far side is mirrored (`sim/pokemon.ts` isAdjacent; Imposter copies `foe.active[length - 1 - position]`): your left is p1a, your right p1b, the opponent's right p2a and the opponent's left p2b, so the foe across from your left Pokémon is the opponent's left. Default: Charizard and Venusaur versus Blastoise and Pikachu at full HP with their Doubles quick moves (the first supported species stands in for one a game lacks), no move chosen and the default Doubles field. All four slots always hold a Pokémon. In 2v2 a **Current HP** of 0 (1v1 still asks for 1 or more) is a Pokémon that fainted before the turn with no replacement left, as pinned Showdown keeps it: its card reads **Fainted** and **0 / {maximum} HP** with no move or target, its Build settings stay editable, it takes no turn, it is no target (the other cards leave it out, and a single foe left is picked as the game does), a spread move into the lone foe does full damage, and its partner gets none of its effects. Its move and target are kept and come back with its HP; a target it was given is kept too, the turn aiming at the other foe meanwhile. With both foes fainted a move at a foe has no target (**No target: both foes have fainted.**). The Moves pane shows it as a disabled choice (**· Fainted**), and Last Respects counts at least its side's fainted Pokémon.
- **Cards.** Each card has the species, form buttons, types, HP, **Change Pokémon**, **Edit HP**, four quick moves, **No move** and **All moves**. A quick move or a Moves-pane row sets that Pokémon's move; a quick move also opens replacement mode for that slot, as in 1v1, and a move from the full list shows as **Move: {name}**. **No move** takes its turn at priority 0 and its own Speed and does nothing, as Splash does, so Protect, Payback and Analytic see a Pokémon that acts. When the move takes one target, **Target** lists the slots its Showdown target allows (Z-Moves and Max Moves use the converted move's); spread, random-target, self, side and field moves state their targets instead (**Earthquake → both foes and Venusaur (ally)**, **Outrage → a random foe**, **Protect · Targets itself**). A target is a slot: it stays when another Pokémon takes that slot. A new move keeps the target while the move offers it, otherwise takes the Moves pane's Pokémon while the pane shows this slot, otherwise the left foe.
- **Turn.** The turn runs in pinned Showdown's order with every random event branched exactly. Each card shows its Pokémon's HP after the moves for the chosen roll, a band from the least to the most HP any outcome leaves, and its KO chance once a move reaches it or it can faint to its own move (recoil, Explosion, Life Orb, a target's Rocky Helmet), which that move's step states. **Low** and **High** are whole-turn outcomes: every damage roll is its lowest (or highest) and every random hit count its fewest (or most), so a fast attacker that fails to knock out on Low can take a hit back, and **Low** can leave a Pokémon with less HP than **High**. The turn panel lists each move in order with each Pokémon it reaches, its damage range and KO chance (a hit count chosen in the move settings is named, **3 hits**, as on the 1v1 card), and **Show move**, which opens Moves for that Pokémon on that move. A Pokémon knocked out before its turn does not move, and a move aimed at a fainted foe goes to the other foe. End-of-turn effects are not applied; the turn facts say so with its other assumptions (every move hits, no critical hits unless **Critical hit** is on, no added effect below 100%). A turn with a mechanic the 2v2 calculation does not model reads **Turn not estimated:** with the reason, then each chosen move's damage at the start of the turn.
- **Build settings and Field conditions.** Four Build settings follow the cards, two per row where the calculator is at least 44rem wide, each named after its Pokémon and position, with the same issue counts, auto-opening and **Fix settings** as 1v1. Plus and Minus read the ally's ability, so the editor states it (**Ally Minun has Minus.**, **Its ally has no Plus or Minus.**, **Ally Minun has fainted.**). **Apply Intimidate to both foes** lowers both foes in pinned Showdown's `adjacentFoes()` order (foe position 0 first: the opponent's right for your Pokémon, your left for theirs) and stores every changed build; with one foe fainted it names the other (**Apply Intimidate to Blastoise (left foe)**), and a fainted Pokémon, or one with both foes fainted, has no Intimidate button. Trace's select offers **Not chosen**; Mega Evolution from a Trace form does not store a copied Intimidate in 2v2. Field conditions keeps weather, terrain, **Critical hit** (every damaging hit of the turn), Gravity and the three rooms, and per side Reflect, Light Screen, Aurora Veil, Tailwind and a **Charge** box for each Pokémon; Helping Hand, Friend Guard, priority-blocking partners, Protect, the spread modifier and Fairy Aura come from the four Pokémon and their moves. **Battle states that cannot be set here** is the 1v1 list.
- **Moves pane.** **Moves for** picks the Pokémon whose learnset the list shows and **Damage into** one of the other three; the list's heading is **Moves**. Its rows are that pair's damage at the start of the turn, before any move, with **Uses to KO**. Analytic, Bolt Beak and Fishious Rend read the turn order from the four chosen actions, as the turn does (Analytic boosts when no other Pokémon still has to move; Bolt Beak and Fishious Rend double when the target still has to move), in these rows and in a not-estimated turn's start rows, so the list has no turn-order choice.
- **Rosters.** From 1280px, **Your team** and **Opponent's team** rails flank the calculator with **Left** and **Right** buttons on each entry; below 1280px each slot's shortcuts are in its Build settings. An entry active in one slot cannot also stand in its ally's (**Active as your left Pokémon.**). A roster pick or a new species clears that slot's move, move contexts and Charge; moves aimed at the slot keep aiming at it.
- **Reset, Swap, game and account.** In 2v2, **Reset** restores the 2v2 defaults, returns to **Moves** and **Average** and clears session build edits, which 1v1 shares; team choices and imported teams stay. There is no **Swap** in 2v2. 1v1 Reset and Swap leave the 2v2 Pokémon as they are (1v1 Reset clears the shared session edits). A game change or an account change resets 2v2 too, and once 2v2 has been opened the game confirmation says so. A team-source change, a paste replaced or removed, or a league or opponent change detaches only the 2v2 Pokémon whose roster entry is no longer offered: each keeps its build as a manual one and loses its move. 2v2 announces a team-source change and a paste imported or removed with 1v1's notice, and names each Pokémon detached (**Your left Pokémon detached from its roster entry; its move and move contexts cleared.**).
- **Loading.** The 2v2 calculation (`app/lib/battle/doubles-turn.ts`) loads the first time 2v2 is opened, with the same loading and retry feedback as 1v1; 1v1 does not load it. After that the 2v2 view stays mounted while hidden, so unfinished text survives switching views. Each view's regions render once as they are hidden and again when shown; in between, the other view's updates neither re-render nor recalculate them (`KeepWhileHidden.tsx`). Loading, error and invalid-settings feedback uses 1v1's wording in both views (`results-feedback.tsx`).

## PokéPaste team imports

Both sides independently support a league roster or an imported team. In a team source panel, select **PokéPaste**, check the displayed **Target game**, choose the **Spread format**, and either **Load link and preview** or paste text and **Preview team text**. The preview shows identity, level, item, ability, nature, six training/stat pairs, ordered moves, retained configuration and diagnostic lines. Edit the text to correct a problem and preview again. **Import team** / **Replace team** applies the team list only; select an entry from the shortcuts or **Change Pokémon → Team Pokémon** to load its build and moves.

- **Explicit units:** Champions exports also label Stat Points `EVs:`. **Champions Stat Points** treats EVs/SPs/Stat Points labels as 0–32 points per stat, 66 total; it never guesses units from small numbers. IV fields cannot be used in this encoding. Explicit `SPs:`/`Stat Points:` in traditional mode is an error, not an EV alias. A retained Champions-encoded draft is visibly incompatible in a native game until original EVs/IVs and their explicit source encoding are supplied.
- **Traditional spreads targeting Champions:** **Traditional EVs/IVs — level-50 equivalent** accepts EVs 0–252 (510 total) and IVs 0–31 (31 when omitted). Each stat converts with `floor((IV + floor(EV / 4)) / 2) - 15`. Thus 252/252/4 EVs becomes 32/32/1 points; unused training capacity is not added automatically. Negative/unrepresentable results fail rather than clamping or replacing low IVs. The target is Champions level 50, not the source game's level-100 stats; explicit non-50 levels must be corrected before that set is usable.
- **Traditional spreads targeting native games:** **Traditional EVs/IVs — native levels** retains original EVs, IVs and explicit levels without conversion. Unlisted EVs default to 0, unlisted IVs to 31, and an omitted level to **100**, not the manual-build default of 50. Original parsed source values are retained separately from the materialized build, so later edits cannot alter the import seed or silently relabel its units.
- **Exact identities and prerequisites:** species/forms resolve only through the target game's bundled catalog and complete aliases. Items, abilities and moves must exist there; abilities and learnsets belong to the exact form. An imported base Pokémon holding a stone stays base until Mega is toggled. Explicit transformed forms must meet their source item/move prerequisites, including prepared Dragon Ascent for Rayquaza-Mega; a learnset entry alone is not an equipped move. Unknown fields, duplicate fields/stat assignments/moves, malformed values and excess moves are reported rather than silently ignored.
- **Configuration, not activation:** recognized `Tera Type:`, `Gigantamax:`, `Dynamax Level:`, `Happiness:`, `Hidden Power:` and gender fields are parsed with strict type/range checks. Valid foreign Tera/Max configuration is retained with an inactive explanation, not discarded or activated. In SwSh, only exact source-declared Gmax aliases (for example `Charizard-Gmax`) resolve to their real species plus stored Gigantamax factor; no arbitrary `-Gmax` suffix is stripped and no zero-weight placeholder becomes a build. Imports never turn on Tera, Dynamax/Gigantamax or Z use.
- **Hidden Power and happiness:** `Hidden Power [Ice]` and its accepted typed name are stored as base Hidden Power plus declared type, preserving source text. Native IVs are not changed to force compatibility; missing/conflicting innate-IV context pauses that attack. Return/Frustration use explicit happiness when supplied. Otherwise happiness normally defaults to 255, while a pasted Frustration set follows Showdown's disclosed default of 0. Type configuration cannot make a move available in a game whose catalog excludes it.
- **Preserved moves and metadata:** all four slots retain imported order, including Status moves and empty slots. They are labelled **Imported from team paste**, not usage defaults. Omitted ability/nature/training use disclosed defaults (an omitted ability is the form's first ability slot, as Showdown's teambuilder fills in); omitted item means no item, even for a form requiring one. New imports start full HP, healthy and with zero stages. Nickname, shiny and `Pokeball:` metadata is cosmetic. As in Showdown, the held item follows ` @ ` with a space on each side, so a nickname such as `M@x (Garchomp) @ Life Orb` keeps its bare `@`, and `@ No Item` means no item. A hand-typed `Garchomp@Life Orb`, which Showdown cannot read, is accepted only when the header as a whole is no Pokémon and a single bare `@` separates an exact Pokémon from an exact item; the preview says so. Confirmed individual or fixed species genders can supply Rivalry context, but an unknown gender is never replaced with the engine's default male.
- **Partial availability:** invalid/unavailable members stay visible but disabled. An import can be applied when at least one member is selectable and there is no team-wide parse error, with explicit selectable/unavailable counts. Catalog coverage warnings may remain selectable for inspection with paused-calculation explanations; invalid form/mechanic prerequisites are errors. Repeated species in different sets keep independent identities; importing the same paste on both roles never shares edits. This is not Species Clause, event-combination or regulation validation.
- **Bounded reads:** team text is limited to 64 KiB and 24 members; packed/JSON teams and multiple exported-team sections are rejected. One optional exported heading is accepted as a label/hint, never as an instruction to change rules. A link read accepts only canonical HTTPS `pokepast.es` paste/raw/json paths (or exact bare-host shorthand), fetches the JSON endpoint directly without credentials/referrer/cache/redirects, times out after 10 seconds and caps response bytes at 128 KiB. Failures offer raw-text fallback. Manual text parsing uses no network; metadata is text, not HTML.
- **Session lifetime and stale work:** applied documents, import seeds, raw drafts and cached edits live in memory only. Fetch/preview/Apply work is scoped to the game/catalog, matchup revision and logical role/source epoch as well as current inputs. Input/encoding/source changes, cancellation, replacement/removal, game changes, Reset and account transitions invalidate stale work. Loading a link never applies a team automatically. Swap follows logical ownership rather than treating Your team as permanently left.

## Game switches, drafts and preparation lifetime

- **Confirmed game change:** while the new bundled runtime loads, the existing game and matchup remain explicitly active. Cancel, a failed load or an obsolete response keeps the current matchup; failure offers **Retry game rules**. Once ready, both builds, base-HP editor text, field/side conditions, prepared moves, active transformations, move contexts, form-restoration state, result browsing state and source-keyed prep caches reset together. The new profile starts with its level-50 Charizard/Blastoise defaults and no selected attack. The current outer tab and damage-roll choice are retained.
- **Failed downloads and focus:** a failed catalog request keeps the current matchup. Browsers and the pinned production chunk loader can retain a rejected download, so **Retry game rules** may fail again without making another request. **Reload calculator** is the explicit recovery fallback: it warns that imported teams, drafts, game selection and preparation will be lost, and requires confirmation. Cancelling that confirmation retains everything; no failure automatically reloads the page. Cancel, Retry and current-load completion return focus to the persistent Battle game selector only when the disappearing control owned focus; obsolete completions do not steal focus from another editor.
- **Documents survive, preparation does not:** game change retains league/opponent choices, each role's League/PokéPaste source mode, original applied text/title/URL/source encoding, and unfinished text/URL/title/encoding drafts. Applied documents are re-parsed from their original text under the new catalog, including dormant pasted teams. Incompatible members remain visible and disabled. Nothing auto-activates a team member, and switching away and back does not resurrect the old edited preparation cache or reverse-convert points. Click a compatible entry to load a fresh import seed.
- **Reset:** stays in the current game and retains raw drafts, team-source choices and original imported sets, but clears edited preparation/cache, active effects and contexts. It restores the default Doubles field, full-HP level-50 manual matchup, Your team on the left, **Moves**, and **Average**. Clicking an imported entry afterward reloads its original materialized set, not its discarded edits; an original native level-100 set remains level 100.
- **Source change or paste replacement/removal:** changing League/PokéPaste mode keeps dormant applied teams, drafts and their prep cache. Replacement/removal prunes only that role's obsolete paste cache. A displaced active set keeps its current build/moves as **Manual**, not as a member of the new source. League refreshes never prune pasted teams.
- **Account boundary:** the first resolved account attaches to existing page preparation rather than erasing edits made while authentication loads. A later sign-out/account replacement clears both roles' documents, drafts, seeds, caches and active preparation and returns the calculator to Champions. Reloading/leaving the page also loses this session state. There is no localStorage/database persistence of calculator builds or imported text.

Runtime identity includes the game, profile version and catalog SHA-256. Roster/cache/import/result ownership also uses the relevant role, source identity and revision/epoch tokens; a same-named species or move in another game is not the same preparation. Cancel, Swap, Reset, unmount and account changes invalidate pending game loads; import requests cannot apply an old-game preview after a switch. No global mutable “current game” changes an already-created matchup.

## Quick-move defaults

New non-imported **Champions** builds use the checked-in **August 2026 Smogon Pokémon Showdown Champions usage** snapshot at **cutoff 1630**, mapped to the current battle format:

| Battle format | Usage ladder | Battles |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Doubles | VGC Reg M-B (`gen9championsvgc2026regmb`) | 1,269,250 |
| Singles | BSS Reg M-B (`gen9championsbssregmb`) | 66,884 |

These are community usage assumptions, not official recommendations or discovered opponent sets. Raw move weights are weighted counts, not percentages. Defaults are selected in this order:

1. Match the exact catalog species/form through its ID, name or explicit engine name. Filter the full source move table to positive finite weights for catalog-known, non-Status moves in that exact Pokémon's proven Champions learnset, then take up to four by descending weight. Canonical move ID breaks ties. Zero base power and engine/context warnings do not exclude a damaging-category move; its normal result limitations still apply.
2. If slots remain (Reg M-C newcomers have no Reg M-B usage at all), add a same-type attack for each of the Pokémon's types that the slots lack: its most-used move of that type in the format's aggregate ranking, from its stronger attacking category (Attack or Sp. Atk, with Attack doubled for a usual Huge Power or Pure Power; either when they are equal), with positive base power and no unsupported reason, and power times accuracy breaking ties among unranked moves. Rillaboom gets Wood Hammer in both formats; in Doubles Salamence gets Dragon Claw and Dual Wingbeat (in Singles its most-used Dragon move is Dragon Tail). These fills are labelled **Suggested**.
3. Fill remaining slots with distinct legal damaging moves from the same-format aggregate ranking. The snapshot keeps the full ranking by summed raw weights across exact-matched species, including moves outside their individual top fours. These fills are labelled **Suggested**, not per-species usage.
4. Fill any remaining slots with unused legal damaging moves in deterministic canonical-ID order, also labelled **Suggested**. Leave slots empty when fewer than four are available. **Ditto starts with four empty slots**, not invented attacks.

Missing form usage is never inferred from its base species, and no fallback bypasses the exact form's proven learnset. The one explicit exception is Maushold-Four, which reuses the snapshot's only Maushold row because the source has no separate Family of Four row and the two forms share their learnset, stats, types and abilities. Manual replacements may choose other legal learnset entries, including status moves; selecting one does not create a damage estimate. Changing Singles/Doubles never overwrites existing prepared moves, manual choices or cached prep. The current format is used only when creating fresh defaults; usage provenance stays attached to the slot that received them.

`data/champions/move-usage.json` records attribution, source URLs/month/format/cutoff, battle counts, compressed-archive SHA-256 hashes, the catalog hash, identity/ranking/fallback policies and coverage. The browser reads this bundled snapshot; there is no runtime usage request or automatic latest-data refresh. Refresh is an explicit maintenance operation described below.

Native games do **not** use Champions usage or claim per-species popularity. Fresh native defaults come first from pinned Showdown's **Random Battle sets** (`data/random-battles/gen7/sets.json`, `gen8/sets.json`, `gen9/sets.json` and, for Scarlet/Violet Doubles, `gen9/doubles-sets.json`): generated sets, not usage statistics. `scripts/build-battle-data.ts` compiles the pinned team generators (`data/random-battles/gen7|gen8|gen9/teams.ts`) from the same verified source and runs each format for 10,000 teams with a fixed Gen 5 RNG seed (1,2,3,4); it then ranks up to four legal damaging moves per form by how often the generator gave them to the form's set keys (keys weighted equally), then STAB, power × accuracy and ID, with at most one Hidden Power, and stores them in each catalog's `randomBattle` block. So the generator's own rules count: a gen 7/8 set's preferred type, forced moves, banned pairs and team-level choices. The manifest records every file's SHA-256, the generator run, the mapping counts and the generated moves a form cannot learn (dropped). Keys map to forms as the generator's `getForme` does (cosmetic and Gigantamax keys stand for their family, a Mega key for its form); a battle-only form without its own key takes its entry forms' generated sets that reach it (its required ability and move), and a cosmetic form its family's. Sword/Shield and USUM publish Singles sets only, which their Doubles also reads; a Scarlet/Violet form missing from one file reads the other's. These slots name their source in the move picker. The same generated sets give each form its **usual ability** for a fresh build: the one they gave it most often (keys weighted equally; Showdown's earlier ability slot on a tie), among its own abilities the engine supports, stored per format as `randomBattle.formats.<format>.abilities` and read from the format its quick moves read. So USUM Cloyster starts with Skill Link (its Icicle Spear and Rock Blast hit five times), Haxorus with Mold Breaker rather than Rivalry, Scizor with Technician and Azumarill with Huge Power. A Mega, Primal or Tera form keeps its own single ability (its key's sets name the entry form's), and a form without sets keeps Showdown's first slot. In Sword/Shield and USUM Doubles this is the Singles sets' ability too, so Beheeyem, Magnezone and (USUM) Watchog start with Analytic, which in Doubles asks for the turn order. Forms without a row (unevolved forms, Totems, Unown letters and a few others) and short rows are filled like Champions: a same-type attack for each missing type by power × accuracy in the stronger category, then coverage by the format's Random Battle ranking with at most two moves of a type, labelled **Suggested**; the fill never suggests recharge, self-KO, charge-turn, delayed, history-based or variable-hit attacks, Hidden Power or Tera Blast. Deterministic canonical-ID order is only the last resort (base Hidden Power only, never its typed copies, which a fresh build's IVs could not use), and remaining slots stay empty. In Ultra Sun/Ultra Moon a row's typed Hidden Power also sets the fresh build's IVs (see Hidden Power below). Z/Max attacks are not assigned directly. Imported sets always keep their ordered slots instead of receiving suggestions.

## Mega, Primal and Ultra form preparation

Stone-based Mega eligibility comes from each selected catalog item's explicit `megaTargets` entry/form pairs, not a Pokémon name prefix or taxonomic `baseSpecies`. In Champions, this keeps Raichu X/Y separate from Alolan Raichu, excludes Galarian Slowbro from Slowbronite, and preserves gender-specific Meowstic and Eternal Floette entry forms. The current Champions catalog has 82 Mega targets from 81 stones across 77 entry forms. All of these pairs have matching base HP and learnsets, so toggling does not heal the Pokémon or overwrite its prepared moves.

Champions examples: Raichu X uses **Raichunite X / Electric Surge**, Raichu Y uses **Raichunite Y / No Guard**, and Mega Banette uses **Banettite / Prankster**. Mega/Z variants of Absol, Garchomp and Lucario use their separate exact stones; the “Mega Z” form label does not activate a Z-Move. **Lucario-Mega-Z calculates with Aura Guard**, which halves damage from contact moves; Mold Breaker ignores it. The pinned champions mod forgot to clear Aura Guard's base-data `Future` tag, so the catalog carries one explicit, self-checking availability override for it (see Champions catalog below).

USUM additionally uses exact source `battleOnly`/`changesFrom` relationships for **Rayquaza-Mega**, **Kyogre-Primal**, **Groudon-Primal** and **Necrozma-Ultra**. Rayquaza needs Dragon Ascent actually prepared and cannot Mega Evolve while holding a Z-Crystal (a directly chosen Rayquaza-Mega, like Keldeo-Resolute (SV/SwSh/USUM) with Secret Sword and Meloetta-Pirouette (SV/USUM) with Relic Song, starts with its required move in quick move 1, marked **Required**; if it is later replaced, Build settings shows the requirement with a quick-move choice that Fix settings focuses and a **Replace with …** button, the summary names the missing move, and the change is announced; after reverting to base Rayquaza, Dragon Ascent is no longer marked Required); its non-Z held item is otherwise retained. Primal forms require the Blue/Red Orb. Ultra Burst starts from Necrozma-Dawn-Wings or Necrozma-Dusk-Mane with Ultranecrozium Z; the original entry form is remembered for reversal. Directly selecting Ultra Necrozma does not prove an earlier fusion/transition history. Required-item alternatives remain explicit, for example USUM Arceus-Bug permits Buginium Z or Insect Plate, while SV retains only Insect Plate.

Only the entry species, ability, ability-activation state and held item are saved for reversal. Nature, level/training, configuration, stages, status, HP and move edits remain current through form switches and return to base. The snapshot travels with the logical Pokémon and its source-keyed session cache, not the left/right position. Manual selection of a different Pokémon, game change and Reset clear this restoration state; detached roster builds keep it as manual prep. Unfinished training/level text survives toggles, just as it survives Swap; selecting a different Pokémon still resets those editors.

These buttons prepare an already-selected form; they do not simulate the evolution turn, entry effects, automatic weather/terrain or a one-Mega-per-team restriction. Field effects remain explicit. Existing unsupported-mechanics and damage-only limitations still apply.

**Intimidate** is an action, not a switch: **Apply Intimidate to the right/left Pokémon** (on any Intimidate holder) resolves it once, as pinned Showdown does when the holder enters or Mega Evolves into an Intimidate form, and writes the result into both Pokémon's stages and items (`app/lib/battle/intimidate.ts`). It follows Contrary, Simple, Guard Dog, Clear Body, White Smoke, Full Metal Body, Hyper Cutter, Inner Focus, Own Tempo, Oblivious and Scrappy (these four block it from Sword/Shield on), Flower Veil on a Grass-type holder (in Scarlet/Violet, one that has Terastallized to Grass; if Intimidate fires on the lead turn, before it Terastallizes, the drop lands), Mirror Armor (the drop goes back to the Intimidate user), Defiant, Competitive, Rattled (Sword/Shield on), Clear Amulet, Adrenaline Orb, White Herb (used up, clearing every lowered stage) and Mirror Herb, and a drop that fails at -6 triggers no reaction. It works on the stages the Pokémon really has, counting the entry boosts the engine adds on every calculation (Intrepid Sword and Dauntless Shield, Download, Wind Rider under Tailwind, Embody Aspect, terrain Seeds), then stores the result without them. So a Zacian holding White Herb goes from +1 to 0 and keeps the herb, as in Showdown. For Wind Rider it assumes Tailwind started before the Intimidate. It assumes the target is not behind a Substitute, which blocks Intimidate. In Doubles a Flower Veil partner would also shield a Grass-type target; there is no slot for it, so the result says so. The target's Neutralizing Gas suppresses it unless the user holds Ability Shield; Magic Room and Klutz suppress the items. Because the change lives in the stages, either Pokémon's Mega Evolution keeps it, the reaction uses the ability the target had when it was intimidated, and a calculation never applies it again (Parental Bond's second strike stays at -1). The result appears under the button (in a status region, so screen readers announce it), naming left and right in a mirror match and counting repeated clicks, since each click applies Intimidate once more. Use the button again when Intimidate fires again, such as Scrafty Mega Evolving. To undo it, edit the stages, select any used-up item again and clear Unburden, which turns on when its holder's item is used up. `tests/unit/calculator-intimidate.test.ts` and scripts under the audit harness check it against real Showdown switch-ins.

## Native battle mechanics

Set **configuration** is separate from an **active transformation**. Holding a Z-Crystal does not request a Z-Move; a Tera type is not Terastallization; Dynamax Level and Gigantamax factor do not transform a Pokémon. Use **Tera**, **Dynamax** or **Gigantamax** by the summary name, or the selected attack's **Use Z-Move** control in **Moves**. Active summary buttons toggle off on a second click. Unavailable controls explain the prerequisite rather than silently changing another setting. Active transformations are part of the combatant's session prep; per-move Z/Stellar/hit context is not a team-wide battle history.

### Ultra Sun / Ultra Moon: Z-Moves and Hidden Power

- A damaging Z request requires the matching generic crystal type, or the exact signature crystal's allowed species and base move. A Mega-Evolved Pokémon cannot use a Z-Crystal, including stone-less Rayquaza-Mega. Assign/select the base move, not the Z attack itself. Conversion resolves before ordinary base-move hit-count/history/failure guards, so the result describes one transformed attack with its effective name/type/power/category.
- The request assumes the team's Z use is still available and the base move has PP. Usage consumption, status-Z boosts/called moves and secondary bonuses are not tracked; Uses to KO counts a damaging Z-Move as one use per battle. A status result such as Extreme Evoboost has no direct-damage estimate; it is not replaced with the base attack's damage. **Weather Ball Z** (Normalium Z) takes the weather's type first, as pinned Showdown does, so in sun it becomes Inferno Overdrive, in rain Hydro Vortex, and so on.
- Base Hidden Power and every exact typed catalog entry, including manual selections, use the known innate IV parity at 60 power. A selected typed entry or configured type inconsistent with those IVs or with each other returns **Needs context**, without rewriting the assigned move ID or inventing an IV spread. Every Hidden Power Z conversion requires **Normalium Z → Breakneck Blitz**, not a crystal of its declared Hidden Power type. The catalog preserves explicit typed source identities, while paste syntax stores the declared type separately from the base move. Hidden Power is not introduced into SwSh/SV by retaining its configuration.
- A fresh Ultra Sun/Ultra Moon build whose Random Battle quick moves include a typed Hidden Power (69 forms, such as Magnezone's Ground) gets that type's usual IVs: pinned Showdown's HPivs, the listed stats 30 and the rest 31 (Ground: 30 Sp. Atk and Sp. Def). Showdown's team validator fills them in for an all-31 set with that Hidden Power below level 100, where Hyper Training cannot keep the 31s (its gen 7 Random Battle generator gives them only to sets with a physical attack; Jolteon's generated Hidden Power Ice set has 0 Attack, which changes no damage here). At the fresh level 50 with no EVs no stat changes; with EVs or a higher level a 30 stat can be 1 lower, as in a real level-50 gen 7 Pokémon with that Hidden Power. The IVs are set only when the build is created (a hand pick, a fresh league pick, a reset or a game switch) and only from all-31 IVs with no innate IVs, no declared type and a level below 100. Later edits, a replaced move, restored session builds and imports are never rewritten. The notice names the side and the stats, and the Hidden Power field and the chooser's help name the type the IVs give (`tests/unit/calculator-usum-hidden-power-ivs.test.ts`).
- **Engine corrections:** a Normalize user's Hidden Power keeps its own type and gets no Normalize boost (pinned Showdown's noModifyType), and Revelation Dance's Z-Move (Normalium Z) is a Normal-type Breakneck Blitz, since Z conversion uses the base move's own Normal type; the engine would retype both. `tests/unit/calculator-native-corrections.test.ts` checks these against Showdown.

### Sword / Shield: Dynamax and Gigantamax

- Dynamax Level is **0–10**, with **10** when unset. Gigantamax additionally requires an eligible catalog species and explicitly enabled factor. With that factor enabled, ordinary Dynamax is rejected: use Gigantamax, or remove the factor to use ordinary Dynamax. Zacian, Zamazenta and Eternatus cannot Dynamax/Gigantamax; unverified incompatible battle forms are also blocked.
- One base move resolves to its actual Max/G-Max attack. A status move resolves to **Max Guard** with no damage estimate, and a transformed attack does not reuse the base move's multihit count. G-Max signatures come from the catalog's exact source mapping, not a suffix guess or engine species hint. Other attack types still use the appropriate ordinary Max move.
- **Type-changing Max conversions:** Weather Ball, Terrain Pulse, Multi-Attack and Techno Blast take their weather, terrain, memory or drive type first, so the Max Move is that type's (Max Geyser for Weather Ball in rain). A Gigantamax user gets its G-Max move only when that type is its signature's: Inteleon's Weather Ball in rain is G-Max Hydrosnipe (fixed 160 power, ignoring the target's ability), Blastoise's is G-Max Cannonade, while Eevee's is Max Geyser. The engine picks the Max Move from the Normal data type, so the adapter finds the converted type with a probe and builds the move with it. Full Belly Morpeko's Aura Wheel becomes Max Lightning, and a Liquid Voice sound move stays Normal (Max Strike), all as in pinned Showdown. **Withheld Max conversions:** Judgment, Natural Gift, Revelation Dance and Hangry Morpeko's Aura Wheel, whose Max Move the engine types differently from Showdown. Where a move is unavailable in the profile it remains unavailable; this list is an adapter guard, not additional availability. These requests never fall back to ordinary damage.
- **Power from HP, boosts or a berry:** Crush Grip and Wring Out (the target's remaining HP), Punishment (the target's raised stages) and Natural Gift (the held berry's type and power; it fails without one) are calculated, since the engine matches pinned Showdown for them.
- The damage and HP preview are for the immediate attack: Max-created weather/terrain, stage changes, G-Max residual damage/healing/status effects, duration, eligibility to activate this turn and team-wide consumption are not simulated there. **Uses to KO** repeats the Max Move for Dynamax's 3 turns, with its stat changes, its weather or terrain and G-Max Wind Rage clearing screens, then the ordinary move once Dynamax ends; G-Max moves that damage each turn (Cannonade, Vine Lash, Volcalith, Wildfire), inflict a random status (Befuddle, Stun Shock) or trap (Sandblast, Centiferno) are **Not estimated**.
- **G-Max Fireball** ignores the target's ability as G-Max Drum Solo and G-Max Hydrosnipe do: the engine misspells it "G-Max Fire Ball" in its list of such moves, so the calculation applies the rule itself (Flash Fire no longer absorbs it; Thick Fat, Heatproof, Fluffy and the like no longer change it).
- **Butterfree correction:** the pinned engine's species hint incorrectly names “G-Max Flutterby”; pinned Showdown names **G-Max Befuddle** and the engine contains that correct attack. The adapter supplies the exact catalog signature through the inspected `@smogon/calc/dist/move` `overrideMove` option, which survives cloning. The manifest records this narrow discrepancy; it does not mark ordinary Butterfree unsupported or forgive other unmatched signatures. No vendored engine species table is rewritten.

### Scarlet / Violet: Tera and Stellar

- Choose **Tera Type** before activating **Tera**. The selected type is passed to the generation-9 engine only while active. The UI keeps original typing visible separately from active defensive typing, so configuring a type alone does not change STAB or defenses.
- **Stellar** retains original defensive types and requires an explicit **first use of this move's type?** choice for each damaging attack context. The calculator does not infer past type use or consume the boost across moves/turns. Tera Blast and other supported immediate effects use the pinned engine with that explicit state, not a new species identity. Terapagos-Stellar is the exception: pinned Showdown never uses up its boost, so no choice is asked. While Stellar-Terastallized, Adaptability does not apply (Stellar replaces the STAB step), and Revelation Dance takes the user's own first type, as Stellar keeps the original types; the engine would apply Adaptability and make Revelation Dance Stellar type.
- **Ogerpon and Terapagos:** as pinned Showdown's terastallize does, a Terastallized Ogerpon takes its mask's Tera form (Ogerpon-Teal-Tera for plain Ogerpon) with Embody Aspect, which raises Attack (Hearthflame), Sp. Def (Wellspring), Defense (Cornerstone) or Speed (Teal) by 1 stage on entry. Terapagos is Terastal from entry (Tera Shift, with Tera Shell) and becomes Terapagos-Stellar (Teraform Zero) when it Terastallizes; Teraform Zero clears the weather and terrain at that moment, so set them only if they were restored afterwards (the note says so when either is set). Under the other Pokémon's Neutralizing Gas, Embody Aspect, Tera Shell and Teraform Zero are suppressed, and the notes say so: the weather and terrain stay. Terapagos's HP, its HP check and the remaining-HP preview use the form it battles as (Terastal, or Stellar when Terastallized), and a Terastallized Ogerpon-Cornerstone's preview is not withheld for Sturdy, which Embody Aspect replaces. Turning Terastallization on or off keeps the damage Terapagos has taken, as pinned Showdown's updateMaxHp does when it changes form: 100/170 becomes 165/235 and back; blank stays full HP, HP that would drop below 1 becomes 1, and HP text that is not valid for the old form is left as typed. With **multiple targets** unticked in Doubles, Tera Starstorm hits one foe without the spread reduction. The Tera-only forms (the Ogerpon -Tera rows and Terapagos-Stellar) need Terastallization turned on. `tests/unit/calculator-sv-special-mechanics.test.ts` checks these against Showdown.
- **Protosynthesis and Quark Drive** activate in sun (not Harsh Sunshine; Cloud Nine and Air Lock remove it, Utility Umbrella does not) or on Electric Terrain, or else from Booster Energy, which is then used up on entry, so Knock Off, Poltergeist and Acrobatics see no item. When the field activates them, the Booster Energy stays held. The boost goes to the highest stat with its stages, counting a Seed used on entry just before, with Wonder Room's defensive-stage swap and Showdown's tie order; the result names the stat. When the other Pokémon has Drought, Orichalcum Pulse, Electric Surge or Hadron Engine, the calculation assumes it set the field as both entered (the battle's leads, or replacements for fainted Pokémon; a switch chosen for the turn runs its entry effects before the next one comes in), so the ability activated before the holder's Seed was used and the Seed's rise is not counted. With its field up, the switch below **Held item** (`itemUsedBeforeField`, model `fieldItemChoice`) sets the order for a field that started after the holder entered, as pinned Showdown resolves it: Booster Energy acts whenever its field is down after the holder has entered, never while Magic Room is up and never after an entry under the room, so **Its Booster Energy was used while Electric Terrain (the sun) was down** (unticked by default), for a holder that entered before its field started or was out when it ended, even if it has started again, leaves no item (Acrobatics doubles, Knock Off gets no boost, Poltergeist fails) and keeps the stat picked then, while a field that has been up since it entered, or that another Pokémon's ability set as they entered together, leaves it held; and when the terrain or weather changes, each Pokémon's ability runs before its item, so **Its Seed (Room Service) was used before Quark Drive (Protosynthesis) activated**, ticked by default unless the other Pokémon's ability set the field as both entered, is unticked for a Quark Drive holder that was out before Electric Terrain started (a move, or a partner's or the foe's Electric Surge or Hadron Engine as it led or switched in later), for a Protosynthesis holder already out in the sun when its Seed's terrain started, and for a Room Service holder already out on its field when Trick Room started; one that switched in with both up used its Seed or Room Service first, and after its field ends and starts again once the item was used, the ability picks again counting the item's change, so both stay ticked. With the other Pokémon's Cloud Nine or Air Lock out, a Booster Energy's switch reads **Its Booster Energy activated Protosynthesis**: unticked, the sun activated Protosynthesis before that ability came in, which used the Booster Energy up with no effect and then ended Protosynthesis (no boost, no item), and that stays so after the sun ends or a Magic Room is set. The Seed switch is offered only when the order changes the boosted stat, and each result states the order it used. The other Pokémon's Neutralizing Gas stops them. Magic Room does not end a boost that Booster Energy started on entry: by default the Booster Energy was used on entry, before the room was set (always true of a lead), and under Magic Room the item switch below **Held item** asks whether that happened; unticked, it entered under the room and its Booster Energy stays unused. The switch is not offered when its own field is up or the other Pokémon's Neutralizing Gas stops the ability, since the item's timing changes nothing then. The engine raises a defending holder's Defense or Sp. Def by 5324/4096 where pinned Showdown uses 5325/4096, which leaves some stats a point low; `app/lib/battle/engine-corrections.cjs` corrects that chain (CommonJS, so it writes to the engine's own module exports) without touching `node_modules`.
- **Intrepid Sword and Dauntless Shield** raise the stage only on the first entry of the battle in Scarlet/Violet, so their switch (on by default) says whether that has happened; Sword/Shield raises them on every entry, with no switch. The target's Intrepid Sword line is shown for Foul Play, which uses its Attack.

Native form/active-mechanic toggles keep base HP text (except Terapagos's Tera toggle, which changes its maximum HP and keeps the damage taken, as above), training and prepared moves, but clear that owner's hits/Z/Stellar contexts and advance its move revision. The selected attack/replacement follows the new owner revision, preventing an old callback from reusing stale context. Native slot replacement also clears that owner's contexts; Champions retains its existing hit-count behavior.

### Base HP and effective Dynamax HP

Current-HP input always stores **base/pre-Dynamax HP**, even while transformed. Blank means full base HP. Effective HP for the summary, damage percentages, KO checks and previews is derived once:

```text
multiplier = (150 + 5 × Dynamax Level) / 100
maximum HP = floor(base maximum HP × multiplier)
current HP = floor(base current HP × multiplier)
```

The multiplier applies only during active Dynamax/Gigantamax, and the base-HP-1 exception remains 1 HP. Turning the transformation off never inverse-converts or rounds the stored input; editing level/EVs/IVs never clamps now-invalid HP. The summary/HP field explicitly distinguish base input from effective HP.

Pinned Showdown's `data/conditions.ts` floors both HP values on Dynamax entry. The pinned engine already floors maximum HP but ceilings current HP, which can even exceed its own maximum. `app/lib/battle/health.ts` narrowly adapts `curHP()` on app-owned generation-8 Dynamaxed instances and their clones to Showdown's floor rule. Base stats, original current HP and `curHP(true)` keep the engine's original API meaning; no installed engine source/formula is changed and no general HP-formula replacement is introduced. This is an entry-state damage snapshot, not a simulation of damage accumulation and later Dynamax exit.

## League matchup preparation

The **My team** selector reads only memberships belonging to the verified signed-in account, using the existing authenticated Supabase client and row-level security. Each option identifies the account's team and its league; the current model has one own membership per league. The first accessible league is selected by name/ID order. The **Opponent** selector contains other members of that league, including those without a finalized roster; no opponent is automatically chosen. Labels disambiguate duplicate team names, while selections continue to use league/member IDs rather than names. Clearing My team also clears the opponent; Refresh preserves valid choices and does not choose a replacement after a deliberate clear or a removed membership.

Team lists use current `drafted_teams`, not historical draft picks. This includes free-agent changes after draft completion. Incomplete drafts, missing/empty rosters, no opponents, no memberships and read errors have separate explanations. **Refresh teams** rechecks membership and current rosters; this is not a live-draft or realtime subscription. Loading/errors do not disable manual calculations. Sign-out/account replacement clears the previous account's roster selections and prep.

- **Click-only activation:** Charizard/Blastoise and manually edited builds stay active until you click a roster Pokémon. Changing league/opponent detaches incompatible roster provenance and labels the retained build **Manual** rather than falsely assigning it to the new team.
- **Species-only defaults:** roster data has no saved nature, ability, item, training allocation, HP or status. A first click uses level 50, Serious nature, the ability its Pool Builder row names (or else the form's usual ability: the most-used one in the selected format's Champions usage statistics, in a native game the one its Random Battle sets give it most often, otherwise Showdown's first ability slot, never the alphabetical first; the Pool Builder's plain Zygarde row names Aura Break, apart from its Power Construct rows; a Pokémon picked by hand, a Mega returned to its base form without saved settings and the default pair get the same), zero training/stages, full HP, healthy status and the catalog's default required item where applicable. Champions uses zero Stat Points; native games use zero EVs and 31 IVs (in USUM, a suggested typed Hidden Power's IVs). These are editable assumptions, not a discovered opponent set. Roster pricing/tier and Pool Builder rules never populate combat stats or choose the battle profile.
- **Session prep:** builds, configuration/active transformations, form-restoration choices and all four prepared moves, including manual choices and usage/Suggested provenance, are remembered when you click away and return to an entry. Cache identity includes the game/profile/catalog hash, league, member, roster/acquisition identity and exact catalog species, not just species name or left/right position, so similarly named Pokémon do not share builds or moves accidentally. The cache is memory-only and disappears on leaving/reloading the page, changing accounts, game change or Reset. Invalid numeric values remain invalid. Exact base current-HP text is saved with the entry, including invalid input; other numeric editors retain parsed invalid values rather than their original spelling when leaving an entry.
- **Direction and field effects:** either Pokémon can attack without Swap; your team's Pokémon can receive damage while still on the left. Swap moves prepared moves, roster shortcuts and ownership labels with the builds. Field/side conditions and per-move hit/Z/Stellar contexts are not cached per roster Pokémon. A different roster selection clears the active attack, replacement mode and move contexts but retains field settings and result filters; re-clicking the active entry is a no-op.
- **Refresh and Reset:** unchanged entries retain build/move prep after Refresh; removed/replaced or ambiguously duplicated entries lose their cached association. Reset keeps dropdown choices, restores your shortcuts to the left, clears all cached builds/moves/provenance, and restores Charizard versus Blastoise with default quick moves and the default field.

Roster names are league pool text, not guaranteed canonical Pokémon IDs. Resolution accepts only complete identities from the currently selected game's pinned catalog or explicit equivalent aliases (for example, Mega Charizard X), preserving gender and form distinctions. It never fuzzy-matches, strips form qualifiers or substitutes a base species. League rosters store Pool Builder display names, so `app/lib/battle/roster-identity.ts` adds one fixed, reviewed table of exact Pool Builder names that the catalog aliases miss. It covers form labels worded differently from Showdown's (**Paldean Tauros (Blaze Breed)**, **Floette (Eternal Flower)**, **Mega Meowstic**, **Calyrex (Shadow Rider)**, **Zacian (Crowned Sword)**, **Ogerpon (Wellspring Mask)**, **Rockruff (Own Tempo)**, **Primal Groudon**) and PokéAPI default forms that the same plain name means differently in Showdown: the Pool Builder's **Minior** is the Meteor Form (Showdown's plain Minior is a Core), and its **Maushold** is Family of Four (Showdown's plain Maushold is Family of Three, which weighs 2.3 kg instead of 2.8 kg and so changes Heavy Slam and Heat Crash). The two **Zygarde (Power Construct)** rows also carry that ability into a fresh roster build. A second, small table accepts the pre-Pool-Builder spelling **Paldean Tauros**, which the Pool Builder itself treats as the Combat Breed. Other spellings the Pool Builder accepts for its rows resolve the same way: it also finds a typed name by its PokéAPI slug (`toPokeApiSlug`) or by the slug without separators, so **Ninetales-Alolan**, **Meowstic (Male)**, **Tauros-Paldea-Blaze-Breed**, **MeowsticMale**, **MeowsticFemaleMega** or a slug itself reach the row's form, and the chooser search finds them too. A slug that names its form as Showdown does resolves directly; a third table maps the rest (mostly PokéAPI default forms such as `lycanroc-midday` and `meowstic-male`) to their rows' display names, and the unit suite checks every Pool Builder row's slug resolves like its display name in every game. A name the first tables give a form keeps meaning that form. A table entry whose form is absent from the selected game is unavailable there rather than falling back to a same-named base form. `tests/unit/calculator-roster-identity.test.ts`, part of `npm test` and `npm run check`, derives each Pool Builder row's exact form independently from its PokéAPI slug and checks it in all four games: every row the Pool Builder offers for a game must resolve to that form, any row that resolves must land on its exact form, and the resolved form's base stats and types must equal the row's (except a few older-game stat changes the test names). A new Pool Builder name the calculator cannot match therefore fails the unit suite. Two Pool Builder rows have no calculator form and stay unresolved: plain Floette in Champions, where only the Eternal Flower exists, and Floette (Eternal Flower) in USUM. Family of Four reuses the plain Maushold row of the Champions usage snapshot, which has no Maushold-Four row; the two forms share their learnset, stats, types and abilities. Roster shortcuts read types, sprites and unsupported notes from the selected game's catalog, and league entries, duplicates included, look up their sprite by the roster's own name. A pasted cosmetic form shows its family's sprite, except Minior's cores, which keep their own (the family's plain Minior is the Meteor Form's artwork; Showdown's plain Minior shows the Red Core). PokéPaste imports keep Showdown's own names and do not use the table. Unknown or ambiguous entries remain visible with a manual-selection explanation. Exact catalog forms with known coverage gaps remain selectable for inspection, but validation pauses unsupported damage. Some leagues contain Pokémon outside the selected snapshot; roster membership does not make them supported or establish team/regulation legality.

Desktop cards reuse the app's decorative sprites, including their loading/error fallback. Sprite lookup does not resolve battle identities or change whether an entry is selectable or supported.

League navigation and Refresh perform authenticated reads. Selecting/editing builds, preparing moves and calculating damage remain local; no roster, team or battle build is written to the database or browser storage.

Move interactions carry the owner's identity and revision; replacements also identify the exact slot and current replacement session. Each successful replacement advances that session while retaining the selected move and editable slot. Form/active-mechanic toggles advance only the transformed combatant's move revision and migrate its active selection/replacement to the new revision, preserving direction while rejecting old callbacks. Champions Mega toggles retain hit counts; native toggles clear move contexts as described above. Stale selection, replacement or context callbacks cannot change a new owner after transformations, Swap, Reset, game/species/roster changes or account/provenance invalidation. Results and HP previews are checked against the current runtime and attack's source and receiver rather than a matching move ID alone, so reversing direction cannot reuse the other owner's result.

## Field effects

All controls describe **already-active effects**, not the moves that set them or their remaining turns. The five shared-effect toggles default off, survive **Swap**, and clear on **Reset**. Side-condition groups are labelled **Left Pokémon’s side** and **Right Pokémon’s side**. Choosing an attack in either direction leaves those conditions in place; Swap makes them travel with their Pokémon. Stored `BattleSide` values remain `attacker`/`defender`, and side-condition keys remain `attackerSide`/`defenderSide`; these identify positions, while the calculation maps them to the selected attack's source and receiver.

- **Game-specific weather:** choose one weather at a time. All profiles offer Sun, Rain and Sand. Champions/SV offer **Snow**; USUM/SwSh offer **Hail**, not Snow. USUM additionally offers **Harsh Sunshine**, **Heavy Rain** and **Strong Winds** for explicit primal-weather context. Sun/Rain modify Fire and Water damage; Sand boosts Rock-type Special Defense, and Snow boosts Ice-type Defense. Hail is not relabelled as Snow and does not gain that Defense boost. Residual weather damage is not part of one use's damage or the HP preview, but **Uses to KO** counts sand and hail damage, and Rain Dish, Dry Skin, Ice Body and Solar Power, at the end of each turn (none under Cloud Nine or Air Lock); form/ability selection never silently sets weather.
- **Helping Hand:** enable on the side of the Pokémon using the selected attack to boost its outgoing damage, whether that Pokémon is on the left or right. Helping Hand on the receiving Pokémon's side remains set for its own attacks but does not boost incoming damage. It needs an ally, so in Singles it is disabled and ignored (pinned Showdown fails a Singles Helping Hand with `[notarget]`); a setting kept from Doubles is named in each result's assumptions.
- **Friend Guard partner:** tick **Partner has Friend Guard** on the receiving Pokémon's side to cut the damage it takes to 75%, as pinned Showdown does. Mold Breaker, Teravolt and Turboblaze ignore the partner's Friend Guard, as do moves that ignore abilities (Sunsteel Strike, Moongeist Beam, Photon Geyser, their Z-Moves, and G-Max Drum Solo, Fireball and Hydrosnipe), and Neutralizing Gas on either battling Pokémon suppresses it; fixed-damage moves such as Seismic Toss are unaffected. With two or more other final damage modifiers (a screen, Life Orb or Expert Belt, Solid Rock, Filter or Multiscale, a resist berry) the result is withheld: the engine combines them in a fixed order, while Showdown orders them by each Pokémon's Speed, which can change the damage by 1 HP. It needs Doubles, so Singles disables and ignores it. The partner's own item (for example Ability Shield) is not modelled.
- **Priority-blocking partner:** tick **Partner has Queenly Majesty, Dazzling or Armor Tail** on the receiving Pokémon's side when its partner has one of them (the label names the ones the selected game has: Queenly Majesty and Armor Tail in Champions, Queenly Majesty in Sword/Shield, Queenly Majesty and Dazzling in Ultra Sun/Ultra Moon). As pinned Showdown's `onFoeTryMove` does, the partner stops any move with positive priority aimed at either Pokémon on its side, so the row shows 0 with the reason: moves with their own priority (Sucker Punch, Aqua Jet, Extreme Speed, Fake Out, Feint, Water Shuriken) and priority from Gale Wings, Triage or Grassy Glide on Grassy Terrain, a spread move included (a Gale Wings Air Cutter stops for both targets). Z-Moves and Max Moves have 0 priority unless Gale Wings raises them (Supersonic Skystrike, Max Airstream), and a move without positive priority is calculated as usual. It never stops a move aimed at its user (Bide) and ignores Gale Wings or Triage a Neutralizing Gas target suppresses. It acts in Showdown's TryMove, before Protect or Max Guard and the move's own checks, so a move that gets through Protect (Feint, Unseen Fist, a Z-Move) is still stopped, the result names the partner rather than Protect (a priority move into Max Guard is 0, not unmodelled), and a stopped move asks no question: no hit count for Water Shuriken, no genders for Rivalry, no Stellar first use; each is asked only when the move gets through. Mold Breaker, Teravolt and Turboblaze ignore the partner's ability (and are then listed as applied), and Neutralizing Gas on either battling Pokémon suppresses it; the partner's own Ability Shield, which keeps it through both, is not modelled. The attacking Pokémon's own partner never blocks its moves. It needs Doubles, so Singles disables and ignores it.
- **Protecting target:** tick **Protecting** on a side when its Pokémon used Protect, Detect or a similar move this turn. Attacks into it are blocked (0 damage) unless they bypass Protect, as pinned Showdown resolves it: moves without its protect flag (Feint, Phantom Force, Shadow Force, Hyperspace Hole and Fury, Hyper Drill, Mighty Cleave, G-Max One Blow and Rapid Flow) hit in full; Future Sight and Doom Desire land at the end of a later turn, unblocked; in the main games Unseen Fist contact moves hit in full (Punching Glove removes a punch's contact, and the target's Neutralizing Gas suppresses Unseen Fist unless the attacker holds Ability Shield). Z-Moves, Max Moves and, in Champions, Unseen Fist or Piercing Drill contact moves deal a quarter of the damage, taken after every other damage modifier as Showdown does (the engine quarters earlier, which is 1 HP off with screens, Multiscale or resist berries, so the app applies the quarter itself); fixed damage such as Seismic Toss goes through a bypass in full, and Guardian of Alola uses its own quarter. Immunities still apply after a bypass, so an immune target takes 0. A Protecting target has already used its move this turn (Protect has +4 priority), so Sucker Punch, Thunderclap and Upper Hand fail, Payback has doubled power, and Analytic boosts the attack in Singles (in Doubles it asks for the turn order, since the other two Pokémon may still move). Damp and Snore's own failures come before Protect. A Dynamaxed target protects with Max Guard, which is withheld.
- **Tailwind:** tick **Tailwind** on a side to double its Pokémon's Speed, which sets Electro Ball, Gyro Ball, Bolt Beak, Fishious Rend and Analytic's turn order. In Scarlet/Violet Wind Rider gets its +1 Attack from Tailwind on its side, added to the stages set in Build settings, except when the other battler's Neutralizing Gas suppresses it (Ability Shield keeps it).
- **Charge:** tick **Charge** on a side when its Pokémon used Charge, doubling its next Electric attack. Charge's exact 2x never changes how the other power modifiers round. It does not stack with Electromorphosis; after Tailwind starts beside a Wind Power Pokémon, tick Charge for it too.
- **Battle states that cannot be set:** Field conditions lists common states the calculator cannot represent and assumes are absent, naming only the abilities and moves the selected game has: Wide Guard, Quick Guard, Mat Block and Crafty Shield; Max Guard (Sword/Shield); a partner's Battery, Power Spot, Steely Spirit or Flower Gift; Ruin abilities, Dark Aura, Aura Break or Neutralizing Gas from a Pokémon other than the two shown; abilities suppressed, replaced or swapped by moves such as Gastro Acid or Skill Swap; Foresight, Odor Sleuth and Miracle Eye; Glaive Rush's drawback; targets in Dig, Dive, Fly, Bounce or Sky Drop; Minimize; grounding or lifting from Smack Down, Thousand Arrows, Ingrain, Roost, Magnet Rise or Telekinesis; stat swaps such as Power Trick; and the order the other Pokémon move in Doubles. The native-game engine reads some of these (Battery, Power Spot, Steely Spirit, Flower Gift, Foresight, Power Trick, Ruin abilities, auras), but the app does not expose them yet.
- **Reflect / Light Screen / Aurora Veil:** enable on the receiving Pokémon's side to reduce incoming damage, independently of left/right position. Screens on the source Pokémon's side remain set for attacks it receives. Singles/Doubles scaling and critical-hit bypass are applied. Veil does not stack with the other screens; an already-active Veil can remain selected without its setup weather (Hail or Snow).
- **Gravity:** grounds airborne Pokémon for Ground attacks and terrain. Gravity-prohibited damaging moves, such as Fly and High Jump Kick, report an explained zero rather than the engine's otherwise positive range. Grav Apple receives its power increase. Displayed accuracy stays the catalog value; Gravity's accuracy changes and hit probabilities are not simulated.
- **Trick Room:** lets the slower Pokémon move first within a priority bracket without changing actual Speed stats, so Electro Ball and Gyro Ball keep using actual effective Speed. It feeds the turn order below. Payback follows its explicit **The target has already moved this turn** toggle, never Speed or Trick Room.
- **Turn order** (Analytic, Bolt Beak, Fishious Rend), as pinned Showdown orders a turn: priority first (the target is assumed to use a priority-0 move), then fractional priority (Lagging Tail, Full Incense and Stall move last in their bracket; an eaten Custap Berry at 1/4 HP, or 1/2 with Gluttony, moves first on the turn it is eaten; Magic Room and Klutz stop the items), then the engine's final Speed with Tailwind, paralysis, items and stages, reversed by Trick Room. Quick Claw and Quick Draw are assumed not to activate, and the result says so. Ultra Sun/Moon (generation 7) keeps the order its actions were sorted in before the turn's first Update (pinned Showdown sorts the queue again after an action only from generation 8), so a Salac Berry eaten there, or the Unburden it starts, counts from the next turn ("The turn order was set before the target's Salac Berry was used (generation 7)."). Negative priority (Avalanche, Revenge, Focus Punch) comes from the move itself, since the engine drops it; Utility Umbrella cancels Chlorophyll and Swift Swim, Dynamax cancels Choice Scarf's Speed, and the other Pokémon's Neutralizing Gas cancels Speed abilities. Future Sight and Doom Desire land after every Pokémon has moved, so Analytic always boosts them. **Analytic** boosts when no other Pokémon still has to move: in Singles that is worked out (Aqua Jet moves first and gets no boost; Avalanche moves last and does), always into a Protecting target, and always with its **The target switches before this attack** condition; in Doubles the other two Pokémon's order is unknown, so it asks, even with that condition ticked (the other two still decide it). **Bolt Beak** and **Fishious Rend** double their power when they move before the target (or the target switched in), which only depends on the target, so Doubles is worked out too. A Speed tie is random in Showdown, so it asks. The choice is **Turn order** in the move settings above the move list (the summary's **Set turn order** goes there), and it overrides the worked-out order, for example when the target uses a priority move. `tests/unit/calculator-turn-order.test.ts` checks these against real Showdown turns.
- **Wonder Room:** swaps unboosted Defense and Special Defense; stages remain attached to their original stat. **Body Press under Wonder Room** uses the attacker's original Defense with its Sp. Def stages, a cartridge quirk shared with Shell Side Arm and Download (Smogon mechanics research by Marty, urkerab and DaWoblefet; Gen 9 cartridge footage for the Defense value). Pinned Showdown calculates the same (its `wonderroom` `onModifyMove` switches Body Press to Sp. Def and `calculateStat` swaps the stat back), and the engine matches it; only Showdown's hint message says Defense stages. One sub-case is withheld: into an effective Unaware (not suppressed by Mold Breaker) that the move connects with, while the attacker has a Sp. Def stage that would count, including one from a terrain Seed, because Showdown's Unaware ignores only Attack, Defense and Sp. Atk stages and keeps that stage, the engine ignores it, and no cartridge test settles it.
- **Magic Room:** suppresses held-item effects without removing the item or changing the selected Mega form. Item-based Speed changes also disappear. A room set later does not undo an item already used, as in pinned Showdown: a terrain Seed on its terrain, Room Service under Trick Room and a Protosynthesis or Quark Drive holder's Booster Energy count as used before the room by default (always true of a lead), with the stage in place and the item gone, so Acrobatics doubles, Knock Off gets no boost and Poltergeist fails. The panel's item switch below **Held item**, shown only when it changes the result, covers a Pokémon that entered under the room (or a terrain or Trick Room that started after it): the item is then still held and does nothing, and it stays held after the room ends (a Seed and Room Service react only to entry and to their terrain or Trick Room starting, and Booster Energy only after an entry the room did not block), so an unticked switch stays on offer without Magic Room. An active Klutz, including one copied by Trace or Imposter, never lets these items act, and then no switch is offered. Suppressed Focus Sash/Focus Band do not block the KO calculation, while effective Sturdy still applies unless bypassed. A suppressed item is still held, as pinned Showdown checks it: under Magic Room, or when Klutz suppresses it, Acrobatics keeps its usual 55 power and Poltergeist still hits (the engine treats a suppressed item as absent, so the app overrides it and says so in the result). Klutz never suppresses Ability Shield, Macho Brace or the Power items, and the other battler's Neutralizing Gas suppresses Klutz itself, so the item then works as usual (the engine drops a Klutz holder's item before it applies the gas, so the app corrects that for every move). More generally, the engine applies Neutralizing Gas only after it has used abilities for Speed, entry boosts and items, and it ignores Utility Umbrella for Chlorophyll, Solar Power, Flower Gift and Swift Swim; the app hands it such a suppressed ability as none.
- **Additional Fairy Aura on the field:** represents another active Pokémon's aura and boosts Fairy-type attacks on either side, including attacks converted to Fairy. It does not stack with Fairy Aura already supplied by either selected Pokémon. Leaving the toggle off does not disable their own Fairy Aura ability. Singles has only the two battling Pokémon, so the toggle is disabled and ignored there. The same applies to the **A partner with Plus or Minus is present** condition, which needs an ally.

Active shared effects are also explained in calculated move details. Engine/data pins and availability are unchanged; these controls do not add unverified aura variants or a full battle simulator.

## Result semantics and limitations

- Damage is uncapped potential HP damage, with percentages of the receiving Pokémon's **maximum HP**. A one-use KO chance uses its HP when the move starts (its **current HP**, after any berry it ate before the move), conditional on the move connecting. Both are effective transformed HP while Dynamax/Gigantamax is active, not the stored base-HP input. The chance does not multiply by accuracy or include end-of-turn damage, healing or hazards; **Uses to KO** (below) covers repeated uses.
- The receiving Pokémon's projected HP number and meter come from the latest unfiltered result for the selected owner/direction, only if the move connects. A calculated row carries its first use's exact outcome (`afterUse`) when it can be worked out: one use from the HP when the move starts, its hits walked as pinned Showdown's hitStepMoveHitLoop walks them (every roll of every hit, each count of a random hit count with its chance, no hit once the target or the attacker has fainted, Focus Sash and Sturdy at full HP on the first hit only, Multiscale and Shadow Shield on the first, and the target's HP berries, Berry Juice, Enigma Berry and Cheek Pouch at each hit's Update), with no end of turn. With it, **Low** and **High** are the HP left by every hit's lowest roll with the fewest hits and by every hit's highest roll with the most; **Average** is the expected HP left over every sequence, rounded to whole HP (a half rounds the damage up); the band on the meter runs from the least to the most HP any sequence leaves. The damage figure is the damage dealt: the HP when the move starts minus the HP left, plus the HP the target regained on that roll's sequence. For Average with healing that can occur it reads as the HP when the move starts and the HP left ("183 → 87 HP"). The healing facts under the HP left ("Sitrus Berry: +45 HP.") are the selected roll's for Low and High, and every one that can occur for Average. Without one (an alternate case such as Fickle Beam's doubled power or a Shell Side Arm tie, a Focus Band, a stat Berry the hits would eat between them, a copied rise that may come before or after the move) the preview is per roll, for one hit: `max(0, currentHP − selectedDamage)` (`max(1, …)` for False Swipe and Hold Back), Low/High the minimum/maximum damage and Average `round(sum(rolls) / 16)` including duplicate rolls, not the midpoint of the two bounds, rounded before subtraction and overkill clamping (HP after mean damage, not the mean of separately clamped HP outcomes, and not necessarily an attainable individual roll). The meter's scale remains maximum HP, not current HP. Builds must be valid, bounds must be consistent nonnegative integers, and the per-roll preview needs a complete fixed/flat distribution. A result still calculated for an earlier HP entry (the recalculation in the background while the HP is typed) shows no one-use outcome. Explicit HP of zero is invalid input, not full HP; a projected zero is allowed but is not a simulated faint or a replacement KO probability.
- Without a one-use outcome, nonzero HP previews are conservatively withheld (except for False Swipe and Hold Back, whose own Showdown onDamage leaves the target 1 HP before Sturdy, Focus Sash or Focus Band can act, so the Sash stays held) for selected Focus Sash, Focus Band or Sturdy, for a Sturdy the calculation settles on (copied by Trace, including one chosen in Build settings, or by Imposter) when the hit's damage reaches the target's HP, multihit/unresolved hit counts, nested/incomplete rolls and unavailable KO semantics ("Remaining HP is withheld for Focus Band."). This remains conservative even if an item/ability would be suppressed or bypassed; the UI does not duplicate the engine's effective survival rules. Valid raw damage and the engine's KO result are retained. A one-use outcome walks Focus Sash and Sturdy, so with one they are previewed: 138–164 damage versus 155 HP with Focus Sash leaves 1–17 HP, with a 0% one-use KO and Uses to KO: Guaranteed 2HKO. Proven calculated zero damage retains the HP the move starts from, whatever the hit count (Super Fang into a Ghost type, Tail Slap into Gengar) and even with survival selections, but for a target ability that takes the move's type and heals a quarter of its base maximum HP once a move (pinned Showdown onTryHit: Water Absorb, Volt Absorb, Dry Skin, Earth Eater; not past Mold Breaker or Protect): "102 → 153 HP" with "Water Absorb: +51 HP.". Unknown/status/unsupported damage never becomes zero.
- Previewing a move or changing the roll does not alter either build or its cached HP, accumulate damage, or change the engine's all-roll KO probability. Slot replacements update prepared-move prep, not input HP or other combat settings. The attacking Pokémon's card shows the HP it has when the move starts (after a berry it ate before the move), whether it is on the left or right; its recoil, drain, retaliation and secondary effects are not shown, and the receiving Pokémon's healing is what the one-use outcome walks. The HP preview is one use; Uses to KO covers repeated uses. Loading, calculator errors, invalid settings, stale owner/direction results and missing/unsupported results immediately remove the numerical projection instead of showing a last-valid estimate. The receiving Pokémon's meter falls back to the HP the move starts from when that build is valid; invalid builds have no HP meter.
- Numeric results assume the attack is executed successfully except for explicitly checked failures/immunities. Per-move details state conditions such as Sucker Punch succeeding, pre-hit Special Attack increases, and first-turn-only attacks. Accuracy shown is the catalog value, not a calculated hit probability.
- Single-hit KO probabilities use the complete fixed or 16-roll distribution. Full-HP Focus Sash and effective Sturdy prevent a single-hit KO. Several hits take the one-use outcome's exact KO chance, and so does a single hit that has none of its own. Without a one-use outcome a multi-hit KO chance is not estimated, nor is Focus Band's (with Focus Band, Uses to KO is **Not estimated** too; it follows multi-hit moves hit by hit). False Swipe and Hold Back have a 0% KO chance whatever the rolls, Focus Band or hit count, and **Never KO** in Uses to KO unless end-of-turn damage can finish the target, which is then counted (USUM and SwSh have both, Scarlet/Violet False Swipe only, Champions neither); their Z-Move (Breakneck Blitz) and Max Moves (Max Strike, or a Normal G-Max move such as G-Max Replenish) lose the effect and can knock the target out, as a note says (`tests/unit/calculator-false-swipe.test.ts`).
- **Uses to KO** repeats the selected move into the same target, one use a turn from its current HP, with both Pokémon staying in and the target doing nothing that changes the count. Each use rolls its own damage, so the count is an exact search over every roll sequence and the state each one leaves. **Guaranteed nHKO** (**OHKO** for one use) is the number of uses that knock the target out whatever the rolls, with the attacker still standing: a move whose lowest roll is 20% of the target's HP, every use the same, is a guaranteed 5HKO. The line under it is the exact chance that one use fewer is enough ("0.53% chance to 4HKO"); for a 2HKO it shows ("53.52% chance to OHKO") only when it differs from the one-use KO chance beside it, as for a multi-hit move or end-of-turn damage. **Counts end-of-turn damage** marks a count that end-of-turn damage can finish (One-use KO leaves it out), and a chance that is not certain never reads 100% (">99.99%"). Details add the fewest uses the best rolls need when that is lower still, or when a chance with no guarantee (below) takes the place of the best rolls' line. When nothing the damage reads changes and the end of each turn only heals the target by less than any use deals, or only damages it by a fixed amount, the uses are independent sums, counted by one convolution (@smogon/calc's exact 2–4-use chances are the same sums). A search that would need too many states or calculations, and Wring Out, Crush Grip and Hard Press when the lowest rolls need more than two uses (their damage reads the exact HP), take the lowest and the highest roll on every use instead, with no chance, and a note says so; the cell then adds "Possible nHKO with the best rolls" when the best rolls need fewer uses. Uses to KO covers every roll, so **Low**, **Average** and **High** never change it. Screen readers hear the tokens as words ("Guaranteed KO in 5 uses").

The Champions engine skips that check (the main games' engine has it), so the app gives the engine Run Away in its place; with the Iron Ball working the ability changes no damage. Under Magic Room the suppressed Iron Ball does nothing and Levitate blocks Ground moves again. `tests/unit/calculator-iron-ball.test.ts` checks this against Showdown.

With the priority, Armor Tail, Queenly Majesty and Dazzling block it and it shows 0 damage into them (or into their partner, with the priority-blocking partner set), unless the attacker's Mold Breaker (or Teravolt / Turboblaze) ignores them, as Ogerpon-Hearthflame's does; an Ability Shield on the holder keeps the block. Helping Hand, terrain boosts, Technician and other abilities and items still change the damage but are not folded into the label. Triple Axel and Triple Kick show their first hit's power with a "Power per hit" line (20, 40, 60). The **Applied** list names only effects that changed the damage: Mold Breaker (or Teravolt, Turboblaze) stays when ignoring the target's ability changed the damage or the KO chance (a note names Sturdy and the like), when it breaks through an intact Disguise, or when it ignores the partner's Friend Guard or lets a hit through the partner's Queenly Majesty, Dazzling or Armor Tail, and Ability Shield stays only when the ability it kept mattered. Notes cover what the engine does silently: a critical hit that Shell Armor or Battle Armor prevents, Infiltrator ignoring a screen, and a terrain Seed used up. Ability conditions are listed for the attacker, and for the target only when they change its Speed or typing (Unburden, Slow Start, Protean, Libero, Imposter). Moves that can fail carry their success condition (Sucker Punch, Thunderclap, Upper Hand and Shell Trap among them; Temper Flare's doubling is its move-settings toggle). `tests/unit/calculator-labels.test.ts` checks these.

The row shows the usual range with the 30% doubled range (and its share of maximum HP) beside it, the details list both sets of rolls, the one-use KO chance weighs the two (70% and 30%), and the HP preview gives the doubled case's damage and remaining HP, at the same roll, after the usual remaining HP. In **Uses to KO** each use rolls its own 30%: the chances weigh every sequence of usual and doubled uses, the guaranteed count takes the usual power every use and the fewest uses the doubled power. `tests/unit/calculator-fickle-beam.test.ts` checks both branches against Showdown.
- **Shell Side Arm** is physical when pinned Showdown's estimate floor(floor(floor(2 × level / 5 + 2) × 90 × Attack / Defense) / 50) beats the same for Sp. Atk and Sp. Def, from stats with their stages only (items, abilities, burn and screens do not count; under Wonder Room each stored defense takes the other's stage), and on a tie Showdown picks at random. The engine agrees whenever the estimates differ, but on a tie it always picks one category, so the app shows the special hit with the physical hit (which makes contact) as a 50% alternate, weighs the KO chance 50/50 and names each hit's applied effects when they differ; when both halves deal the same damage a note says so. An intact Ice Face blocks the physical half. In **Uses to KO** each use flips its own coin: the chances weigh every sequence of special and physical hits (only a physical hit makes contact, for Rough Skin or Rocky Helmet), and the guaranteed count and the fewest uses take the weaker and the stronger half every use. Max Ooze has no coin flip. `tests/unit/calculator-shell-side-arm.test.ts` checks this against Showdown with the coin forced both ways.
- **Count-based power** (`app/lib/battle/count-moves.ts`), as pinned Showdown computes it: **Supreme Overlord** gains 10% power per ally that had fainted when the Pokémon entered, up to 5, set with **Allies fainted before it entered** in Build settings; **Last Respects** has 50 power plus 50 per fainted party member; **Rage Fist** has 50 plus 50 per hit the user has taken, up to 350 (Champions resets that count when the user switches out, other games keep it all battle). Their counts default to 0, the start of a battle, are set in the move settings above the move list, and every result states the count it used. A Supreme Overlord boost with two or more other power boosts (for example Helping Hand and a type-boosting item, or Knock Off into a held item) is withheld: the engine combines them in a different order from Showdown's handler priorities, which can change the damage by 1–2 HP. **Beat Up** needs its party chosen in the move settings (**Set party** in the summary goes there): the other party members that are not fainted and have no status, up to 5 (this team's Pokémon are listed first, a Mega or other battle-only form as its team form). It hits once for the user and once per chosen member, each hit at 5 + one tenth of that Pokémon's base Attack from its team form (a Mega uses its base form), without a Parental Bond strike or the Tera 60-power floor. Each hit is calculated separately, so Beat Up is withheld into effects that change damage after the first hit: Multiscale or Shadow Shield at full HP, Stamina, Weak Armor, a Colbur Berry that halves the first hit, and Seed Sower holding a Grassy Seed off Grassy Terrain (a critical hit or Unaware that ignores the Defense rise is still calculated). A Kee Berry and Anger Shell act only after the whole move and Tera Shell resists every hit, so those match. `tests/unit/calculator-count-moves.test.ts` checks these against Showdown.
- Other history-dependent moves (for example Counter, Fling, Fury Cutter) get **Needs context**, not guessed damage. A verified Z/Max conversion is resolved first and evaluated as that transformed attack, not its ordinary base move. Rivalry needs both confirmed individual/fixed species genders. Missing context without an input is never assigned a convenient default.
- Protean/Libero require the visible **Protean (Libero) unused since switch-in** assumption, with unchanged typing. Clearing that condition suspends calculations: previously changed typing is not modeled. Apart from the explicit supported Tera/form controls and Mimicry, arbitrary type changes, ability replacements, item consumption histories and unexposed ally effects are outside v1.
- Aegislash with Stance Change attacks using verified Blade Forme stats. Select Mimikyu-Busted for attacks after Disguise has broken; intact Disguise is not simulated. It takes the first hit that connects: Normal, Fighting and Dragon moves into Mimikyu, and moves that fail, show 0 instead. Select other battle states directly rather than assuming transformation timing.
- **One-target Expanding Force:** on Psychic Terrain a grounded user's Expanding Force has 1.5x power and targets both foes. With **Multiple targets hit** off in Doubles it hits only the selected target, without the spread reduction, as Showdown does when the target's partner is absent or has fainted. A partner still on the field keeps the spread reduction even when it protects, is immune or is semi-invulnerable, because Showdown decides the reduction from the targets before those checks (`trySpreadMoveHit`), so the toggle stays on then. The one-target line appears only when the engine actually retargets the move (a grounded user on Psychic Terrain); for other users the toggle changes nothing. The engine always treats the retargeted move as spread, so the adapter keeps the engine's copy of the move single-target, which retains the engine's own terrain power and Doubles screen rules.
- Other unsupported special-damage mechanics are explicitly withheld. Fling's item eligibility/consumption is not verified. Source or engine data gaps remain visible instead of being silently mapped to another Pokémon/move.
- The adapter compensates for narrowly verified pinned-engine gaps: Gale Wings and Triage priority (Triage gives draining moves +3; Gale Wings never raises Hidden Power, which Showdown queues as the Normal dex move) is supplied before priority-immunity checks, while a Terastallized user's Tera/Stellar 60-power floor still reads the move's own priority, as Showdown's does (Gale Wings Peck and item-holding Acrobatics reach 60); Double Shock and Burn Up fail for a user without the Electric or Fire type (its Tera type, or its own types under Stellar); effective Damp prevents explosive moves, and Gravity blocks prohibited attacks. These checks use the resolved attack rather than incorrectly applying an ordinary move's failure to its Z/Max conversion. The Showdown-floor Dynamax current-HP adapter and exact Butterfree G-Max signature correction are described above. Field-specific and special-form/conversion cases without verified support are withheld rather than patched with guessed damage. The vendored engine's battle source is unchanged.

This is a pinned snapshot, not a claim that every current game mechanic or ranked ruleset is supported. Catalog coverage reports **data compatibility**, not exhaustive mechanics verification.

### Champions catalog

The Champions generator resolves `Dex.mod('champions')`, including form/learnset inheritance and availability. Each contributing learnset must have explicit Champions provenance; a `9M` marker alone is not enough. It never substitutes unrestricted Gen 9 movepools. Pinned source retrieval is an explicit maintenance operation; there is no runtime source request.

The current catalog has **396 species/forms/states, 515 moves, 216 assigned abilities and 166 items**; its species contain **23,769 proven learnset entries**. Cosmetic forms are their base Pokémon in battle: the Vivillon patterns, Florges colours, Furfrou trims and all eight Alcremie flavours share their parent's engine identity (Showdown's `getAlias`, the same rule as the native pipeline), because pinned Showdown declares them cosmetic and their battle data is equivalent. Vivillon-Fancy and Vivillon-Pokeball keep their own engine entries. The loader also enumerates source-declared `cosmeticFormes` that `species.all()` omits (Florges colours, Furfrou trims, Alcremie-Salted-Cream), as the native pipeline does; each one has its parent's move pool. Their exact selected/imported IDs, names and preparation remain unchanged. Catalog identities take precedence over shared engine aliases, so plain Alcremie remains unambiguous. Smogon's usage statistics count cosmetic forms under their family (the source has only Vivillon, Alcremie, Florges and Furfrou rows), so a cosmetic form reads its family's usage row for quick moves and its usual ability, and a pasted one shows its family's sprite. This is not a generic missing-form fallback: every other data-mismatch gate still applies to the exact form, and Gmax entries are not added.

**Ability availability override.** The champions mod clears the base-data `isNonstandard: "Future"` tag for every other new Champions ability (Dragonize, Eelevate, Fire Mane, Mega Sol, Piercing Drill, Spicy Spray) but not for Aura Guard, the only ability of Reg M-C's Lucario-Mega-Z. The simulator ignores that tag in battle, the Reg M-C validators accept Lucario holding Lucarionite Z, and the engine implements Aura Guard, so `ABILITY_AVAILABILITY_OVERRIDES` in `scripts/lib/champions-data/transform.ts` treats it as available. Every build re-proves the override: it fails if the ability stops being assigned to an available species, if the source no longer carries the stale tag, or if the engine lacks the ability, so the override has to be removed once upstream fixes the mod. The catalog's coverage notes record it. This is not a general rule that `Future` abilities are available.

Known data gaps are Battle Bond, Pound and Growth's type mismatch. Battle Bond is absent from the engine, and Battle Bond Greninja is not obtainable in Champions: pinned Showdown's team validator treats it as Greninja-Bond, which is unavailable there, and the build issue says so. Pound is absent from the engine, and no Champions Pokémon learns it. Only Growth has a type mismatch: Champions makes it Grass, and the engine still has it as Normal. Per-entry reasons and source metadata are in `data/champions/catalog.json` and `manifest.json`.

`scripts/build-battle-data.ts` and `scripts/lib/battle-data/` generate separate `catalog.json`, `manifest.json`, `LICENSE.pokemon-showdown.txt` and `LICENSE.damage-calc.txt` files in each directory:

These counts reflect the pinned native availability including applicable DLC, not National Dex, competitive tiers or a ranked-regulation allowlist. Current native catalogs have no blocking species/move/ability/item **data-parity** gaps; the recorded Butterfree hint discrepancy and runtime mechanics exclusions still apply. Zero data gaps is not exhaustive calculation verification.

- **Complete inheritance:** the native compiler resolves `gen7 → gen8 → base`, `gen8 → base`, and the `gen9` alias of base through the actual pinned Dex. It includes historical species/move/ability/type-chart patches rather than loading one mod directory or using current base data indiscriminately.
- **Actual move-pool semantics:** the generator calls `species.getFullLearnset` and `species.getMovePool(id, false)`. Generations 7/8 retain eligible transfer sources with the pinned HM/regional-evolution rules; generation 9 applies HOME-reset/current-generation rules with the source's egg/regional-evolution exceptions. Sketch also follows the real Dex implementation. Native inherited tables are valid provenance: the Champions explicit-mod declaration requirement is intentionally not applied. At these pins, emitted native learnset move tables originate in base `data/learnsets.ts`; the gen8 learnsets patch only adds Vivillon-Fancy event metadata.
- **USUM learnset validation:** Showdown's move pool unions a form's whole learnset chain, so forms that cannot breed or transfer (cap Pikachu, Totem forms, Greninja-Ash and -Bond, event Vivillon) would inherit moves they cannot know in USUM. The build checks every USUM (form, move) pair with pinned Showdown's team validator (`gen7anythinggoes`, against the out-of-battle form, or any of a battle-only form's entry forms) then applies validateSet's event-only move-origin check: an event-only form cannot know a move whose sources are all older than every one of its events, which removes Zygarde-10%'s Rock Smash, Secret Power and Strength. The catalog withholds the 80 pairs these reject, across 18 species, listed per species in the manifest; the manifest's `resolvedMoves` and `resolvedMovePoolSha256` still describe the raw pinned `getMovePool`, and the source gate pins the exact rejected pairs. The validator's random-number dependency is stubbed in the disposable build; learnset checks use no randomness.
- **Signature moves:** Hyperspace Fury (Hoopa-Unbound) and Aura Wheel (Morpeko) fail for any other user, as pinned Showdown's onTry does, so a Hoopa or a Smeargle shows 0 with the reason.
- **Honest scope:** a resolved movepool is an individual-move union, not proof of mutually compatible event moves, ability/level restrictions, origin marks, exclusive combinations or team/regulation legality. Unavailable moves and directly assigned Z/Max attacks are excluded from species pools; exact engine-supported typed Hidden Power entries expand only a source-proven base Hidden Power pool.
- **Exact identities:** source IDs, names, types, stats and weight are retained. An exact engine ID takes precedence. Aegislash's explicit Shield base-form mapping and source-proven cosmetic aliases are documented exceptions; no generic `baseSpecies` fallback fills an engine gap. The loader also enumerates source-declared `cosmeticFormes` absent from `species.all()`. SwSh/SV therefore include all eight non-default Alcremie flavor forms, including Salted-Cream; Champions enumerates the same way. Gmax placeholder rows are not real species: exact placeholder names become `gmaxNames` on the directly referenced real species, and eligible source identities carry their exact signature move ID.
- **Determinism and provenance:** each manifest records catalog SHA-256, engine generation/package/revision/archive and snapshot hashes, requested/resolved Showdown mod and complete ancestry, compiler-input hashes, license hashes, per-species learnset-provider/pool hashes, aliases/proofs, excluded identities, omitted unavailable item alternatives/Z users and coverage diagnostics. Ordering is deterministic; there are no timestamps or machine-specific paths in generated output. Source differences are surfaced, not repaired with guessed engine/base-form data.

### Future Champions mechanics gate

Parsing or retaining a Tera type, Z/Max-related source field or foreign-game configuration does not enable that mechanic in Champions. Neither another profile's support nor generic generation-9 engine behavior is a valid generation-0 fallback. Before changing the Champions capability flags, require pinned Champions rules/provenance, validated source/engine availability and prerequisites, an explicit generation-0 adapter/context/HP contract, independent mechanic reference cases and negative/stale-state regressions. Update the profile/catalog identity and review all existing Champions fixtures, including exact Alcremie identities and the non-converting Stat Point path. Until that gate is satisfied, future Champions mechanics stay inactive or explicitly unsupported.

## Maintenance and checks

Ordinary `npm ci`, `npm run check` and production builds use the checked-in engine tarball, catalogs and Champions move-usage snapshot; they do not require ignored upstream source archives, run source generation or download upstream rules. The normal native unit suite checks committed catalog/manifest/license integrity, parity with the installed engine, small independent transform fixtures, and missing/corrupt archive behavior using temporary fixture bytes.

Fresh upstream extraction and full regeneration are a separate, explicit **`npm run test:battle-data:source`** gate (`vitest.battle-data.config.ts`, `tests/source/battle-data.test.ts`), outside the ordinary unit-test include. This strictly offline suite requires both exact pinned archives already present in `node_modules/.cache/champions/`. Missing/corrupt archives fail the gate explicitly; there is no conditional skip or hidden download fallback. It preserves the complete real-source regressions: no-network generation, Windows license extraction, byte-exact assets/licenses/manifests, compiler/provider provenance, actual Dex transfer/HOME/Sketch pools, reordered-input determinism, cosmetic/form proof and engine-mismatch diagnostics. Committed-asset consistency alone does not replace this source-integration gate.

Calculations, quick-move defaults, slot replacements and build edits make no data-source/network/database calls once their bundled code is loaded. Optional league shortcuts use existing authenticated membership/current-roster reads; explicit PokéPaste link imports use the bounded credential-free reads above. App authentication still applies; no calculator migration or database seed is required. Imported-set seeds are in-memory preparation, not database seeding.

Run maintenance commands from the repository root:

```sh
# Full application gates; no upstream source-cache prerequisite.
# These commands are not a statement that the current full gates have passed.
npm run check
npm run build

# Focused portable native unit tests; works after npm ci without source archives.
npm test -- tests/unit/battle-data.test.ts

# Explicit offline source-integration gate; both pinned archives are required.
# Missing/corrupt archives fail, rather than downloading or skipping tests.
npm run test:battle-data:source

# Native catalogs: strictly offline; same verified-source-cache prerequisite.
npm run data:battle
npm run data:battle -- --check

# Equivalent direct commands recorded in the native manifests.
npx --no-install tsx scripts/build-battle-data.ts
npx --no-install tsx scripts/build-battle-data.ts --check

# Explicit engine/Champions maintenance only; may fetch pinned, SHA-256-checked sources.
npm run prepare:champions-engine
npm run data:champions
npm run data:champions -- --check

# Opt-in Champions usage refresh/check; may fetch the pinned usage archives.
npm run data:champions:move-usage
npm run data:champions:move-usage -- --check
```

The native builder regenerates all three profiles together; `--check` byte-compares every catalog, manifest and license without rewriting assets. It verifies complete cached archive bytes and installed-engine provenance before loading source, stages the verified bytes in a fresh private `battle-data-*` directory, compiles the full required native Dex graph and removes temporary output afterward. It does not trust an old mutable extraction, modify the installed engine or update dependencies. For Windows/OneDrive portability, only the engine archive's regular root `LICENSE` is extracted: this is the pinned `calc/LICENSE → ../LICENSE` target, so no `calc/LICENSE`/`calc/README.md` symbolic links need to be created. License contents/hashes stay unchanged and full archive verification still precedes extraction. Only the native commands promise strict offline behavior; the explicit Champions maintenance commands retain their own pinned-download behavior.

`data:champions:move-usage` runs `scripts/import-champions-move-usage.mjs`. It verifies compressed source hashes, bounded downloads/decompression, source metadata and schema, and the catalog digest before deriving the snapshot. `--check` compares derived output without rewriting it; it can still download pinned archives missing from the local cache. The same command and its `--check` also write and verify `data/champions/training-usage.json` (the Training tab's per-species moves of every category, items, spreads and abilities, from the same hash-checked Doubles archive); `move-usage.json` and the calculator do not read it. To adopt another month, deliberately review/update the importer's pins and metadata, regenerate the checked-in snapshot, and rerun relevant gates. Neither command runs automatically at install, build or runtime, and that usage snapshot remains Champions-only.

Verified source archives/extractions and temporary compiler output live under ignored `node_modules/.cache/champions/`. Restore the exact verified archives before the explicit source-integration gate or an offline native regeneration/check; a missing cache is not permission to fetch latest source. Ordinary unit/check gates need neither those archives nor an old extraction. After intentionally changing engine/source pins or package version, rebuild/reinstall the local dependency as needed, regenerate the affected catalogs and usage provenance, and rerun all gates. Review newly exposed mechanics instead of treating engine name presence as proof of support. Update independent reference fixtures only after checking the new upstream source directly.

Portable unit suites cover engine dispatch/arithmetic, profile-isolated training/availability, committed-data integrity, deterministic transform fixtures, form/mechanic prerequisites, imports and stale lifecycle actions, base/effective HP, conditional damage/KO semantics and UI helpers/markup. The explicit source gate separately proves fresh-source provenance and complete regeneration. Data parity and these maintenance instructions are **not final full-check/build/browser results**. A release still requires the current complete gates, the offline source-integration gate and signed-in interactive browser checks across game switches, import/configuration controls, responsive layouts, themes and keyboard/accessibility behavior; unit tests do not replace those checks.
